Meanings and usage

The core definition and examples are always included. Additional notes appear only when they are accurate and useful.

Meaning 1

Discover or establish

transitive verb /dɪˈtɝːmɪn/ neutral to formal; very common in academic and technical writing
🔊 Hear transitive verb pronunciation

to discover, calculate, or establish a fact, amount, cause, or answer through careful examination

In simpler words: find out or establish

Typical pattern: determine + noun, whether or if, question word, or that-clause

Natural examples

  1. Researchers are trying to determine whether sleep quality affects long-term memory.
  2. The laboratory test can determine the concentration of lead in the water.
  3. Investigators could not determine the exact cause of the equipment failure.
  4. Scientists examined the fossils to determine their approximate age.
  5. The survey was designed to determine how residents use public transportation.
  6. Further evidence is needed to determine which explanation is more accurate.
  7. The committee will determine whether the proposal meets the legal requirements.
  8. Analysts used satellite images to determine the extent of forest loss.

Nuance and implication

Central nuance: Determine suggests reaching a sufficiently definite answer by using evidence, measurement, calculation, or authority.

Usual implication: The answer was previously uncertain and requires investigation or judgment.

Compared with “estimate”: To estimate is to produce an approximate value. To determine is to establish an answer considered sufficiently definite for the purpose.

Grammar notes

How to use it
This verb normally needs a fact or question after it.
What can follow it
Use a noun, as in determine the cause; whether or if, as in determine whether it works; or a question word, as in determine how it happened.
Position in a sentence
It is frequent in passive forms such as The age was determined by carbon dating.

Useful patterns

determine + cause, effect, amount, extent, value, or identity

Use this when an investigation establishes a fact.

Example: The study sought to determine the extent of the damage.

determine whether or if + clause

Use this when choosing between possible answers.

Example: Additional trials will determine whether the treatment is safe.

determine how, why, when, or where + clause

Use a question word to identify the information being investigated.

Example: Researchers hope to determine why the population declined.

Common wrong usage

Wrong: The test determined about the cause of the infection.

Correct: The test determined the cause of the infection.

Do not use about before a direct object such as the cause.

Wrong: Scientists determined that how the material changed.

Correct: Scientists determined how the material changed.

Use either that or a question word such as how, not both together.

Meaning 2

Control or shape an outcome

transitive verb /dɪˈtɝːmɪn/ neutral; common in academic explanations of causes and outcomes
🔊 Hear transitive verb pronunciation

to control, cause, or strongly influence the nature or result of something

In simpler words: control or decide what happens

Typical pattern: factor + determines + outcome or whether/how clause

Natural examples

  1. Access to reliable transportation can determine whether workers accept jobs far from home.
  2. Soil quality largely determines which crops can grow in a region.
  3. A society's institutions help determine how resources are distributed.
  4. Early experiences do not completely determine a person's future behavior.
  5. The availability of water may determine the size of the settlement.
  6. Several interacting genes determine the color of the flowers.
  7. The final examination will determine half of each student's grade.
  8. Economic conditions often determine the pace of construction.

Nuance and implication

Central nuance: In this sense, determine means having a decisive or powerful effect, not discovering information.

Usual implication: The subject is an important causal or controlling factor, although context may show that other factors also matter.

Compared with “influence”: Influence means affect to some degree. Determine often suggests a stronger effect that fixes or decides the outcome.

Grammar notes

How to use it
The controlling factor is the subject, and the result is placed after determine.
What can follow it
Use a noun or a clause beginning with whether, how, what, or which.
Position in a sentence
Adverbs such as largely, partly, ultimately, and directly can show how strong the effect is.

Useful patterns

factor + determine(s) + outcome

Use this to connect a controlling cause with its result.

Example: Temperature determines the rate of the chemical reaction.

determine whether or how + clause

Use this when a factor controls whether or how something happens.

Example: Household income may determine whether a student can remain in school.

be determined by + factor

Use the passive when the result is more important than the controlling factor.

Example: The final cost is determined by the amount of material used.

Common wrong usage

Wrong: Weather determines on the success of the harvest.

Correct: Weather determines the success of the harvest.

Determine takes a direct object; do not add on.

Wrong: Income is determined from several social factors.

Correct: Income is determined by several social factors.

Use by to introduce the controlling factor in a passive sentence.

Meaning 3

Make an official decision

transitive verb /dɪˈtɝːmɪn/ formal when it means make an official decision
🔊 Hear transitive verb pronunciation

to make an official or authoritative decision about something after considering the facts

In simpler words: decide officially

Typical pattern: determine + noun, question-word clause, or that-clause

Natural examples

  1. After reviewing the evidence, the board determined that the investigation should continue.
  2. The court will determine the appropriate level of compensation.
  3. Officials determined that the building should remain closed until repairs were complete.
  4. The committee is responsible for determining who receives the scholarship.
  5. The agency determined the final boundaries of the protected area.
  6. The judge will determine the appropriate penalty after hearing both sides.
  7. The panel determined that a second vote was necessary.
  8. Local authorities determine the standards that new buildings must meet.

Nuance and implication

Central nuance: Determine is more formal than decide here and often suggests that a court, committee, agency, or rule has authority to settle the matter.

Usual implication: The decision is reached after facts, standards, or evidence have been considered.

Compared with “decide”: Decide is the ordinary general word. Determine is more formal and is common when an authority settles a question.

Grammar notes

How to use it
Use determine followed by a noun, question-word clause, or that-clause.
What can follow it
Common forms include determine the outcome, determine who qualifies, and determine that action is needed.
Position in a sentence
Do not confuse this verb with the adjective determined in be determined to.

Useful patterns

determine + question word + clause

Use this for an official decision about a person, time, place, or method.

Example: The panel will determine who is eligible for funding.

determine that + clause

Use this to report a formal conclusion or decision.

Example: The court determined that the regulation was valid.

determine + noun

Use this when an authority decides an outcome, amount, boundary, or standard.

Example: A national panel determines the minimum safety standard.

Common wrong usage

Wrong: The panel determined about the final amount.

Correct: The panel determined the final amount.

Determine takes the matter being decided as a direct object.

Wrong: The panel determined about who would receive the award.

Correct: The panel determined who would receive the award.

A question-word clause follows determine directly.

Morphology

Word family

Only common and educationally useful family members are included.

countable or uncountable noun

determination

/dɪˌtɝːməˈneɪʃən/

the process of establishing something, an official decision, or the strong quality of continuing toward a goal

Relationship: This noun covers both establishing an answer and being firmly resolved.

Pattern: determination of + fact, or determination to + verb

Usage note: Its exact meaning depends strongly on context.

  1. The determination of the sample's age required further testing.
  2. Her determination helped her complete the demanding program.

adjective

determined

/dɪˈtɝːmɪnd/

having made a firm decision and refusing to give up

Relationship: This adjective describes firm intention or effort.

Pattern: be determined to + base verb, or a determined + effort

Usage note: Pronounce the final syllable as /mɪnd/, not like the verb mind.

  1. She was determined to improve access to rural schools.
  2. The team made a determined effort to recover the missing data.

Meaning relations

Synonyms and antonyms

Only clear and useful relationships are included.

Close synonyms

establish

verb · Discover or establish

Shared meaning: Both can mean reaching a firm factual conclusion.

Difference: Establish emphasizes showing that a fact is true. Determine can also involve calculating or identifying an answer.

Example: The evidence established that the document was authentic.

decide

verb · Make an official decision

Shared meaning: Both can refer to choosing an outcome after consideration.

Difference: Decide is the usual everyday term. Determine is more formal when an authority reaches a decision.

Example: The committee decided to postpone the vote.

Precision

Words learners may confuse

determine

verb versus adjective

Why it is confusing: The forms look similar but perform different grammatical jobs.

Key difference: Determine is a verb. Determined is usually an adjective when it describes firm intention.

determine: The test will determine the cause.

determine: The researcher was determined to find the cause.

Word history

Etymology

Only confidently documented details are shown.

Origin

Determine comes from Latin determinare, meaning to limit or set boundaries.

Development

The idea of setting limits developed into fixing an answer, controlling a result, or settling a decision.

Memory link

To determine something is to bring uncertainty to a defined endpoint.
