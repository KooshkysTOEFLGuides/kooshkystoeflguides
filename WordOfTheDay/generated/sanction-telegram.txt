🔹 Word: sanction
🔊 Primary pronunciation: /ˈsæŋkʃən/

🧠 Meaning 1
Part of speech: countable or uncountable noun
Pronunciation: /ˈsæŋkʃən/
Definition: official approval or permission for an action, decision, or activity

Examples:

1. The project could not proceed without the official sanction of the local authorities.
2. The policy was introduced with the sanction of the university administration.
3. Military action would require government sanction.

🧠 Meaning 2
Part of speech: countable noun
Pronunciation: /ˈsæŋkʃən/
Definition: an official penalty or restriction used to punish a person, organization, or country for breaking a rule or behaving in an unacceptable way

Examples:

1. The government imposed economic sanctions on the country.
2. The organization may face sanctions for violating environmental regulations.
3. International sanctions severely limited the country's access to foreign markets.

🧠 Meaning 3
Part of speech: transitive verb
Pronunciation: /ˈsæŋkʃən/
Definition: to officially approve or give permission for an action, decision, or activity

Examples:

1. The committee sanctioned the use of additional funds for the project.
2. The government refused to sanction the proposed military operation.
3. The university sanctioned the construction of a new research facility.

🧠 Meaning 4
Part of speech: transitive verb
Pronunciation: /ˈsæŋkʃən/
Definition: to officially punish a person, organization, or country for breaking a rule or behaving in an unacceptable way

Examples:

1. The agency can sanction companies that repeatedly violate safety regulations.
2. Several officials were sanctioned for their involvement in the illegal activity.
3. Governments may sanction foreign organizations involved in prohibited trade.
