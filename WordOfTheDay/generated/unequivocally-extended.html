Dictionary guide

Meanings and usage

The core definition and examples are always included. Additional notes appear only when they are accurate and useful.

Meaning 1

Clearly and without any doubt

adverb /ˌʌnɪˈkwɪvəkəli/ formal; common in academic, legal, political, journalistic, and professional writing
🔊 Hear adverb pronunciation

in a completely clear and definite way that leaves no doubt about what is meant, believed, or true

In simpler words: very clearly and with no doubt

Typical pattern: unequivocally + state, reject, support, condemn, show, or demonstrate

Natural examples

  1. The researchers stated unequivocally that the evidence did not support the original theory.
  2. The university unequivocally rejected the accusation of discrimination.
  3. The data show unequivocally that average temperatures have increased over the study period.
  4. The mayor unequivocally supported the proposal to expand public transportation.
  5. Medical experts have unequivocally condemned the use of the unsafe treatment.
  6. The court ruled unequivocally in favor of the workers.
  7. She answered unequivocally that she would not participate in the project.
  8. The results do not unequivocally prove that the new method is superior.

Nuance and implication

Central nuance: The word is stronger than simply clearly. It emphasizes that there is no ambiguity, hesitation, or uncertainty in the statement, position, or conclusion.

Usual implication: It is often used when the writer or speaker wants to emphasize a firm position or a conclusion that they consider especially clear.

Compared with “apparently”: Apparently suggests that something seems to be true but may still be uncertain. Unequivocally indicates that there is no such uncertainty or ambiguity.

Grammar notes

How to use it
Use it to describe a verb, adjective, or complete statement when you want to emphasize complete clarity or certainty.
What can follow it
It often comes before verbs such as reject, support, condemn, demonstrate, show, state, and deny. It can also come before adjectives such as positive or clear.
Position in a sentence
It commonly appears before the main verb, as in The committee unequivocally rejected the proposal. With verbs such as state or say, it can also appear after the verb, as in She stated unequivocally that she opposed the plan.

Useful patterns

unequivocally + reject, support, condemn, or deny

Use this to emphasize that a position is firm and leaves no uncertainty.

Example: The organization unequivocally condemned the attack.

state or say unequivocally that + clause

Use this when someone expresses a position in completely clear terms.

Example: The director stated unequivocally that the policy would remain in effect.

show or demonstrate unequivocally that + clause

Use this when evidence is considered clear enough to leave no reasonable doubt about a conclusion.

Example: The experiment did not demonstrate unequivocally that the treatment was effective.

unequivocally in favor of or opposed to + noun

Use this to express a completely clear position for or against something.

Example: The committee was unequivocally in favor of stricter safety standards.

not unequivocally + verb

Use this when the evidence or statement is not clear enough to support a definite conclusion.

Example: The available evidence does not unequivocally establish a causal relationship.

Common wrong usage

Wrong: The evidence unequivocally maybe supports the theory.

Correct: The evidence may support the theory, but it does not do so unequivocally.

Unequivocally expresses complete clarity or certainty, so combining it directly with maybe creates a contradiction.

Wrong: The government gave an unequivocally response.

Correct: The government gave an unequivocal response.

Use the adjective unequivocal before a noun. Unequivocally is an adverb.

Wrong: She unequivocal rejected the proposal.

Correct: She unequivocally rejected the proposal.

Use the adverb unequivocally to describe the verb rejected.

Wrong: The results unequivocally suggest that the treatment might possibly work.

Correct: The results suggest that the treatment might work.

Unequivocally is usually inappropriate when the rest of the statement deliberately expresses uncertainty.

completely clear and definite, with no possibility of being understood in more than one way

Pattern: unequivocal + support, rejection, answer, evidence, statement, or position

  1. The committee gave its unequivocal support to the proposal.
  2. The evidence did not provide an unequivocal answer to the question.

adjective

equivocal

/ɪˈkwɪvəkəl/

not completely clear or definite and therefore open to more than one interpretation

Relationship: Unequivocal is formed by adding un- to equivocal, reversing its meaning.

Pattern: equivocal + result, answer, response, evidence, or statement

Usage note: Equivocal is formal and often describes evidence, statements, or results that are unclear or inconclusive.

  1. The results were equivocal and did not clearly support either explanation.
  2. His equivocal response made it difficult to determine whether he supported the proposal.

Meaning relations

Synonyms and antonyms

Only clear and useful relationships are included.

Close synonyms

unambiguously

adverb · Clearly and without any doubt

Shared meaning: Both mean in a way that leaves no uncertainty about the intended meaning.

Difference: Unambiguously emphasizes that something has only one clear interpretation. Unequivocally can also emphasize firmness, certainty, or lack of hesitation in a position or statement.

Example: The instructions unambiguously state that all applicants must submit two references.

categorically

adverb · Clearly and without any doubt

Shared meaning: Both can emphasize a firm statement with no qualification or uncertainty.

Difference: Categorically is especially common with strong statements, denials, and refusals. Unequivocally is broader and can also describe evidence, support, conclusions, and positions.

Example: The minister categorically denied the allegation.

definitively

adverb · Clearly and without any doubt

Shared meaning: Both can express a high degree of certainty.

Difference: Definitively often emphasizes that a question has been settled conclusively or finally. Unequivocally emphasizes that the meaning, evidence, or position is completely clear.

Example: The new evidence definitively established the age of the artifact.

clearly

adverb · Clearly and without any doubt

Shared meaning: Both can describe something expressed or demonstrated in an easy-to-understand way.

Difference: Clearly is much more common and can simply mean in an understandable or obvious way. Unequivocally is stronger and more formal, emphasizing that no serious ambiguity or doubt remains.

Example: The results clearly indicate a decline in water quality.

Direct or useful antonyms

ambiguously

adverb · Clearly and without any doubt

Opposition: Ambiguously means in a way that allows more than one interpretation, while unequivocally means in a completely clear way that leaves no such uncertainty.

Example: The rule was ambiguously worded and produced several competing interpretations.

equivocally

adverb · Clearly and without any doubt

Opposition: Equivocally means in an unclear or deliberately noncommittal way, while unequivocally means clearly and without hesitation or ambiguity.

Usage note: Equivocally is formal and much less common than unequivocally.

Example: The spokesperson answered equivocally and avoided taking a definite position.

Precision

Words learners may confuse

unequivocal

adverb versus adjective

Why it is confusing: The two forms have the same core meaning but different grammatical roles.

Key difference: Unequivocal is an adjective that describes a noun or follows a linking verb. Unequivocally is an adverb that usually describes a verb, adjective, or entire statement.

unequivocally: The committee unequivocally rejected the proposal.

unequivocal: The committee gave the proposal an unequivocal rejection.

equivocally

adverb

Why it is confusing: The words differ only by the negative prefix un-, but their meanings are opposites.

Key difference: Unequivocally means clearly and without doubt. Equivocally means unclearly, ambiguously, or without taking a firm position.

unequivocally: The organization unequivocally supported the reform.

equivocally: The organization responded equivocally and did not clearly support either side.

Word history

Etymology

Only confidently documented details are shown.

Origin

Unequivocally is formed from unequivocal and the adverb ending -ly. Unequivocal combines un-, meaning not, with equivocal, meaning ambiguous or open to more than one interpretation.
