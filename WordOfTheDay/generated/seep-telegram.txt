🔹 Word: seep
🔊 Primary pronunciation: /siːp/

🧠 Meaning 1
Part of speech: intransitive verb
Pronunciation: /siːp/
Definition: to flow or pass slowly in small amounts through tiny openings, a surface, or a material

Examples:

1. Contaminated water began to seep through cracks in the storage tank.
2. After several days of rain, moisture seeped into the basement walls.
3. Oil from the damaged pipeline seeped into the surrounding soil.

🧠 Meaning 2
Part of speech: intransitive verb
Pronunciation: /siːp/
Definition: to spread, enter, escape, or become known slowly and gradually

Examples:

1. News of the discovery gradually seeped out despite efforts to keep it confidential.
2. The values of the movement seeped into mainstream political debate.
3. A sense of uncertainty began to seep into the team's discussions.
