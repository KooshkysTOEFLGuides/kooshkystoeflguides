Meaning 1

Goods kept for use or sale

countable or uncountable noun /ˈɪnvənˌtɔːri/ neutral to formal; very common in business, economics, manufacturing, retail, and supply-chain contexts
🔊 Hear countable or uncountable noun pronunciation

the goods, materials, or products that a business or organization has available or stored for future use or sale

In simpler words: the products or materials a business has in stock

Typical pattern: inventory of + products or materials; keep or hold + inventory; in inventory

Natural examples

  1. The company reduced its inventory after demand for the product declined.
  2. Retailers often increase their inventory before major shopping seasons.
  3. The factory keeps enough raw materials in inventory to avoid interruptions in production.
  4. Poor inventory management can cause both shortages and unnecessary storage costs.
  5. The store has a large inventory of electronic devices.
  6. Manufacturers try to avoid keeping excessive inventory when demand is uncertain.
  7. The company sold much of its remaining inventory at a discount.
  8. Supply chain problems made it difficult for businesses to maintain adequate inventory.

Nuance and implication

Central nuance: Inventory refers to goods or materials that are currently available rather than goods that have already been sold or consumed.

Usual implication: Businesses often need to balance having enough inventory to meet demand against the cost of storing too much.

Compared with “sales”: Inventory consists of goods a business still has available, while sales refer to goods that have already been sold.

Grammar notes

How to use it
Inventory can be uncountable when referring generally to a company's stock of goods, but it can also be countable when referring to a particular stock or range of items.
What can follow it
It often appears with verbs such as keep, hold, maintain, reduce, increase, manage, sell, or replenish.
Common preposition
Use in inventory or in stock for goods currently available. Use inventory of when naming the type of goods.
Noun use
In business English, inventory is often uncountable: The company has too much inventory. A large inventory is also possible when referring to a particular quantity or collection of stock.

Useful patterns

keep or hold inventory

Use this for goods or materials a company stores for future use or sale.

Example: The retailer keeps relatively little inventory in its smaller stores.

inventory of + products or materials

Use this when naming what kinds of items a business has available.

Example: The warehouse contains a large inventory of replacement parts.

in inventory

Use this for goods currently being held by a business or organization.

Example: The company still has several thousand units in inventory.

reduce or increase inventory

Use this when a business changes the quantity of goods it stores.

Example: The manufacturer reduced inventory after orders began to fall.

inventory management

Use this for the process of controlling how much stock a business keeps and when it replaces it.

Example: Effective inventory management can reduce storage costs.

Common wrong usage

Wrong: The company has many inventory in its warehouse.

Correct: The company has a lot of inventory in its warehouse.

When inventory means stock in general, it is often uncountable, so use a lot of rather than many.

Wrong: The store increased its inventories before the holiday season.

Correct: The store increased its inventory before the holiday season.

When referring generally to the store's total stock, the singular or uncountable form inventory is more natural.

Wrong: The products are on inventory.

Correct: The products are in inventory.

Use in inventory, not on inventory.

Meaning 2

Detailed list of items

countable noun /ˈɪnvənˌtɔːri/ neutral to formal; common in museums, science, administration, environmental studies, archaeology, and resource management
🔊 Hear countable noun pronunciation

a complete and detailed list of the items, resources, or materials that a person or organization has

In simpler words: a complete list of what is available

Typical pattern: an inventory of + objects, species, resources, equipment, or materials

Natural examples

  1. The museum created an inventory of all the artifacts in its collection.
  2. Researchers prepared an inventory of plant species found in the protected area.
  3. The laboratory maintains a detailed inventory of its chemicals and equipment.
  4. Before the building was renovated, staff compiled an inventory of its historical features.
  5. The agency developed an inventory of available water resources.
  6. Archaeologists made an inventory of the objects recovered from the site.
  7. The school keeps an inventory of computers and other electronic equipment.
  8. Officials used the inventory to determine which supplies were missing.

Nuance and implication

Central nuance: An inventory is usually systematic and comprehensive rather than a quick or informal list.

Usual implication: The purpose is often to record what exists, where it is, how much there is, or what condition it is in.

Compared with “sample”: A sample contains only some items selected from a larger group, while an inventory aims to record all or nearly all relevant items.

Grammar notes

How to use it
This meaning is countable. Say an inventory for one detailed list and inventories for more than one.
What can follow it
It is commonly followed by of and the category being recorded.
Common preposition
Use an inventory of something: an inventory of equipment, an inventory of species.
Noun use
The singular normally needs a determiner such as an, the, or this.

Useful patterns

an inventory of + items

Use this for a detailed record of a collection or group of things.

Example: The researchers compiled an inventory of native plant species.

create or compile an inventory

Use this when people systematically produce a detailed list.

Example: The museum compiled an inventory of objects damaged in the flood.

maintain an inventory

Use this when an organization keeps a list accurate and up to date.

Example: The laboratory maintains an inventory of hazardous chemicals.

detailed or comprehensive inventory

Use these adjectives when emphasizing completeness or detail.

Example: The survey produced a comprehensive inventory of the region's wetlands.

Common wrong usage

Wrong: Researchers made an inventory for the species in the forest.

Correct: Researchers made an inventory of the species in the forest.

Use an inventory of something when naming the items included in the list.

Wrong: The museum prepared inventory of its paintings.

Correct: The museum prepared an inventory of its paintings.

This meaning is countable, so the singular needs a determiner such as an.

Wrong: An inventory is just one example taken from a larger collection.

Correct: An inventory is a detailed list of the items in a collection.

A sample contains only selected examples; an inventory aims to record the collection systematically.

Meaning 3

Make a detailed list

transitive verb /ˈɪnvənˌtɔːri/ formal; common in administration, science, museums, archaeology, business, and resource management
🔊 Hear transitive verb pronunciation

to make a detailed list of the items or resources in a place or collection

In simpler words: make a complete list of

Typical pattern: inventory + objects, equipment, resources, species, or supplies

Natural examples

  1. Museum staff inventoried thousands of objects before moving the collection.
  2. Researchers inventoried the plant species found in each study area.
  3. The organization inventoried its equipment to determine what needed to be replaced.
  4. Archaeologists carefully inventoried every object recovered from the site.
  5. Employees spent several days inventorying the contents of the warehouse.
  6. The agency inventoried available water resources throughout the region.
  7. Before closing the laboratory, staff inventoried all remaining chemicals.

Nuance and implication

Central nuance: As a verb, inventory means systematically identify and record what is present.

Usual implication: The process often includes counting, describing, locating, or assessing the condition of the items.

Grammar notes

How to use it
This verb normally needs a direct object: inventory the equipment, inventory the collection.
What can follow it
Common objects include equipment, supplies, objects, resources, species, assets, and collections.
Position in a sentence
The -ing form is inventorying, with the y kept: inventorying the supplies.

Useful patterns

inventory + equipment or supplies

Use this when systematically recording what equipment or materials are available.

Example: Staff inventoried the emergency supplies before the hurricane season.

inventory + collection

Use this when recording the contents of a museum, archive, or other collection.

Example: The museum began inventorying its entire archaeological collection.

inventory + species or resources

Use this in scientific or environmental contexts when recording what exists in an area.

Example: Scientists inventoried the species living in the wetland.

Common wrong usage

Wrong: The researchers inventoried about the species in the forest.

Correct: The researchers inventoried the species in the forest.

Inventory is transitive as a verb, so put the thing being recorded directly after it.

Wrong: The staff was inventory the equipment.

Correct: The staff was inventorying the equipment.

After was, use the -ing form inventorying.

Meaning relations

Synonyms and antonyms

Only clear and useful relationships are included.

Close synonyms

stock

countable or uncountable noun · Goods kept for use or sale

Shared meaning: Both can refer to goods that a business currently has available.

Difference: Stock is the common everyday business word for goods available for sale or use. Inventory is especially common in American business English and often emphasizes the quantity being stored and managed.

Example: The store does not have enough stock to meet current demand.

supply

countable noun · Goods kept for use or sale

Shared meaning: Both can refer to materials kept for future use.

Difference: A supply is an amount of something available for use, especially something that may be consumed. Inventory more often refers to goods or materials systematically held and tracked by an organization.

Example: The hospital maintains an emergency supply of medicine.

catalog

countable noun · Detailed list of items

Shared meaning: Both can be organized lists of items.

Difference: A catalog is an organized list, often with descriptions and sometimes intended for users or customers. An inventory is mainly a record of what items actually exist or are currently held.

Example: The library published an online catalog of its rare books.

list

countable noun · Detailed list of items

Shared meaning: Both can record a group of items.

Difference: List is the general word. Inventory usually suggests a systematic and relatively complete record of possessions, resources, or items in a collection.

Example: The researcher made a list of the samples collected that day.

Direct or useful antonyms

shortage

countable noun · Goods kept for use or sale

Opposition: Inventory is the stock of goods or materials available, while a shortage is a situation in which there is not enough of something to meet demand.

Usage note: This is a useful contrast rather than a perfect dictionary antonym.

Example: The factory experienced a shortage of essential components.

Precision

Words learners may confuse

stock

noun

Why it is confusing: The two words overlap strongly when referring to goods available for sale.

Key difference: Inventory can mean the goods a business holds, especially in American business English. Stock can have the same meaning, but it also has several unrelated meanings, including shares in a company.

inventory: The retailer has too much inventory in its warehouses.

stock: The retailer has too much stock in its warehouses.

catalog

countable noun

Why it is confusing: Both can refer to organized lists of objects.

Key difference: An inventory records what items are actually present or owned. A catalog is an organized list or presentation of items and may include things that are available, described, or offered rather than physically present.

inventory: The museum completed an inventory of all objects in storage.

catalog: The museum published a catalog describing the exhibition.

invent

verb

Why it is confusing: The words begin similarly but have different modern meanings and uses.

Key difference: Inventory refers to stock or a detailed list, and as a verb means make such a list. Invent means create or design something new.

inventory: Staff inventoried all the equipment in the laboratory.

invent: The engineer invented a new type of sensor.

Word history

Etymology

Only confidently documented details are shown.

Origin

Inventory comes through Medieval Latin and French from a word referring to a detailed list of goods or possessions.

Development

The meaning expanded from the written list itself to the goods or stock recorded on that list.

Memory link

An inventory originally focuses on recording what you have; today the word can mean either the list or the actual goods on that list.
