Meanings and usage

The core definition and examples are always included. Additional notes appear only when they are accurate and useful.

Meaning 1

Fit together closely

intransitive and transitive verb /ˈdʌvˌteɪl/ neutral to formal; common in academic, professional, organizational, and analytical writing
🔊 Hear intransitive and transitive verb pronunciation

to fit together closely and successfully, or to make two things fit together in this way

In simpler words: fit together very well

Typical pattern: dovetail with + idea, evidence, plan, schedule, or goal

Natural examples

  1. The results of the two studies dovetail with each other and support the same conclusion.
  2. The new training program was designed to dovetail with employees' existing responsibilities.
  3. Her explanation dovetails neatly with the evidence presented earlier.
  4. The historical records dovetail with archaeological evidence from the same period.
  5. The two departments adjusted their schedules so that their activities would dovetail.
  6. The author's economic argument does not fully dovetail with the social evidence discussed later.
  7. The researchers tried to dovetail their different methods into a single investigation.
  8. The proposed reforms dovetail closely with the university's long-term goals.

Nuance and implication

Central nuance: Dovetail suggests more than simple similarity. The different parts complement one another and fit together in a useful or logical way.

Usual implication: The things being compared often support, reinforce, or coordinate with one another.

Compared with “conflict”: Things that dovetail fit together successfully, while things that conflict are inconsistent or interfere with one another.

Grammar notes

How to use it
It is often used without a direct object and followed by with, as in The findings dovetail with earlier research. It can also be used with an object when someone deliberately combines things.
What can follow it
Common subjects and objects include findings, evidence, theories, plans, schedules, goals, policies, and activities.
Common preposition
Use dovetail with when saying that one thing fits well with another.
Position in a sentence
Adverbs such as neatly, closely, perfectly, and well often appear with dovetail.

Useful patterns

dovetail with + noun

Use this when two ideas, findings, plans, or activities fit together well.

Example: The new evidence dovetails with the earlier findings.

dovetail neatly or closely with + noun

Use an adverb to emphasize how well two things fit together.

Example: The policy dovetails closely with the government's environmental goals.

dovetail + activities, plans, or methods

Use this when people deliberately coordinate different things.

Example: The two teams dovetailed their research activities to avoid unnecessary duplication.

dovetail into + larger whole

Use this when separate parts combine smoothly into a broader plan or system.

Example: The individual projects dovetail into a broader regional development strategy.

fail to dovetail with + noun

Use this when two ideas or plans do not fit together as expected.

Example: The theory fails to dovetail with several important observations.

Common wrong usage

Wrong: The new findings dovetail to the previous research.

Correct: The new findings dovetail with the previous research.

Use dovetail with when two things fit together well.

Wrong: The two theories are dovetail each other.

Correct: The two theories dovetail with each other.

Use dovetail with each other rather than be dovetail each other.

Wrong: The evidence dovetails similarly with the theory because both are identical.

Correct: The evidence dovetails with the theory because the two fit together well.

Dovetail does not mean identical. It means that different things fit together successfully.

Wrong: The manager dovetailed with the two schedules.

Correct: The manager dovetailed the two schedules.

When a person deliberately coordinates two things, those things can be direct objects of dovetail.

Meaning 2

Tightly fitting woodworking joint

countable noun /ˈdʌvˌteɪl/ neutral; mainly used in woodworking, craftsmanship, archaeology, and descriptions of furniture construction
🔊 Hear countable noun pronunciation

a type of joint in which projecting pieces of wood fit tightly into matching spaces in another piece

In simpler words: a strong interlocking wood joint

Typical pattern: cut, make, or use + dovetails

Natural examples

  1. The carpenter used dovetails to join the sides of the wooden drawer.
  2. Well-made dovetails can create a strong joint without relying entirely on nails or screws.
  3. Archaeologists found furniture fragments containing carefully cut dovetails.
  4. Traditional cabinetmakers often used dovetails because the joint resists being pulled apart.
  5. The wooden box was assembled with several hand-cut dovetails.
  6. The shape of each dovetail allows two pieces of wood to lock together firmly.
  7. Students in the woodworking class practiced cutting dovetails by hand.

Nuance and implication

Central nuance: The projecting part is wider at the end than at its base, creating an interlocking shape that resists being pulled apart.

Usual implication: The physical way the pieces fit together gave rise to the figurative verb meaning of fitting together successfully.

Grammar notes

How to use it
Use a dovetail for one joint or projecting piece and dovetails for more than one.
What can follow it
It often appears with words such as joint, drawer, cabinet, wood, cut, and hand-cut.
Noun use
This is a countable noun, so the singular normally needs a, an, or the.

Useful patterns

a dovetail joint

Use this for the complete interlocking woodworking joint.

Example: The cabinet is held together with traditional dovetail joints.

cut dovetails

Use this for making the interlocking shapes in wood.

Example: Experienced woodworkers can cut dovetails by hand.

hand-cut dovetails

Use this for dovetails made manually rather than by a machine.

Example: The antique drawer contains finely made hand-cut dovetails.

Common wrong usage

Wrong: The carpenter made a dovetail with two boards together.

Correct: The carpenter joined the two boards with a dovetail joint.

Use with a dovetail joint when naming the type of joint used to connect pieces of wood.

Wrong: The drawer has dovetail to make it stronger.

Correct: The drawer has dovetails to make it stronger.

Dovetail is countable, so use the plural when referring to several joints or interlocking parts.

Meaning relations

Synonyms and antonyms

Only clear and useful relationships are included.

Close synonyms

correspond

intransitive verb · Fit together closely

Shared meaning: Both can describe agreement between findings, ideas, or information.

Difference: Correspond means match or be similar in a relevant way. Dovetail more strongly suggests that separate things complement each other and fit together into a coherent whole.

Example: The survey results correspond closely with earlier estimates.

align

intransitive or transitive verb · Fit together closely

Shared meaning: Both can describe plans or goals that work well together.

Difference: Align often means bring ideas, goals, or activities into agreement or the same direction. Dovetail suggests that different parts complement each other and fit together closely.

Example: The new policy aligns with the organization's long-term goals.

mesh

intransitive verb · Fit together closely

Shared meaning: Both can mean that different parts work successfully together.

Difference: Mesh is an informal to neutral synonym meaning work or fit together successfully. Dovetail is often more formal and suggests a particularly neat or complementary fit.

Example: The two teaching methods mesh well together.

integrate

transitive or intransitive verb · Fit together closely

Shared meaning: Both can describe combining separate elements successfully.

Difference: Integrate means combine parts into a larger whole. Dovetail emphasizes how well those different parts fit or coordinate with one another.

Example: The researchers integrated several sources of data into one model.

Direct or useful antonyms

conflict

intransitive verb · Fit together closely

Opposition: Things that conflict are inconsistent or interfere with one another, while things that dovetail fit together successfully.

Example: The witness's account conflicts with the physical evidence.

clash

intransitive verb · Fit together closely

Opposition: Things that clash do not combine or work well together, while things that dovetail complement one another.

Example: The two departments' schedules clash on several important dates.

Precision

Words learners may confuse

overlap

verb

Why it is confusing: Both can describe relationships between activities, ideas, or areas of study, but overlap emphasizes shared content rather than complementary fit.

Key difference: Overlap means share some of the same area, time, features, or content. Dovetail means fit together successfully, often because different parts complement one another.

dovetail: The two research methods dovetail well because each provides information the other lacks.

overlap: The two courses overlap because they cover several of the same topics.

align

verb

Why it is confusing: Both are frequently used when discussing ideas, plans, goals, and organizational activities that work well together.

Key difference: Align emphasizes agreement in direction, position, or goals. Dovetail emphasizes different parts fitting together neatly and complementing one another.

dovetail: The new evidence dovetails with earlier findings.

align: The department changed its priorities to align with the university's strategy.

Word history

Etymology

Only confidently documented details are shown.

Origin

Dovetail comes from the woodworking joint whose projecting pieces resemble the spread tail of a dove.

Development

Because the pieces of a dovetail joint fit tightly together, the word later developed the figurative meaning of ideas, plans, or activities fitting together successfully.

Memory link

Imagine two pieces of wood locking neatly together: ideas that dovetail also fit together neatly.
