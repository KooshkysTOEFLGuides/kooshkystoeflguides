Meanings and usage

The core definition and examples are always included. Additional notes appear only when they are accurate and useful.

Meaning 1

Cause to change direction

transitive verb /dɪˈflɛkt/ neutral to formal; common in science, engineering, physics, and technical descriptions
🔊 Hear transitive verb pronunciation

to cause something that is moving to change direction

In simpler words: make something turn away

Typical pattern: deflect + object + away from or toward + direction

Natural examples

  1. The protective shield is designed to deflect small pieces of flying debris.
  2. Earth's magnetic field helps deflect charged particles from the Sun.
  3. The angled surface deflects wind away from the entrance.
  4. The barrier can deflect waves before they reach the harbor.
  5. Engineers added a panel to deflect heat away from sensitive equipment.
  6. The curved structure helps deflect rainwater toward the drainage system.
  7. The material was strong enough to deflect some of the impact.

Nuance and implication

Central nuance: The object continues moving, but its path is changed by a force, surface, barrier, or other influence.

Usual implication: The change may protect something, redirect energy, or alter where a moving object eventually goes.

Compared with “stop”: To stop something is to end its movement. To deflect it is to change its direction while it continues moving.

Grammar notes

How to use it
This verb normally needs the moving object or force after it.
What can follow it
It is commonly followed by nouns such as light, radiation, wind, water, debris, particles, heat, or an object in motion.
Common preposition
Use away from when showing what the object is being redirected away from, as in deflect heat away from the engine.
Position in a sentence
The thing causing the change normally comes before the verb, and the moving object comes directly after it.

Useful patterns

deflect + object

Use this when something causes a moving object or force to change direction.

Example: The shield deflected the incoming particles.

deflect + object + away from + noun

Use this when showing what is being protected from the moving object or force.

Example: The device deflects heat away from the battery.

be designed to deflect + noun

Use this for equipment or structures created to redirect something.

Example: The wall was designed to deflect strong winds.

Common wrong usage

Wrong: The shield deflected away the particles.

Correct: The shield deflected the particles away.

Place the object directly after deflect.

Wrong: The barrier deflected the water to hit the building.

Correct: The barrier deflected the water away from the building.

Use away from when the purpose is to redirect something so that it does not hit a particular place.

Meaning 2

Change direction

intransitive verb /dɪˈflɛkt/ neutral to formal; common in physical descriptions and technical contexts
🔊 Hear intransitive verb pronunciation

to change direction after hitting something or being affected by a force

In simpler words: turn in a different direction

Typical pattern: deflect off + surface or deflect to + direction

Natural examples

  1. The ball deflected off a defender and entered the goal.
  2. Some of the incoming radiation is deflected by particles in the atmosphere.
  3. The stream of water deflected to the side when it struck the metal plate.
  4. The projectile deflected upward after striking the hard surface.
  5. Light can deflect as it passes through certain materials.
  6. The ball deflected off the post and rolled across the field.
  7. The beam deflected slightly when the equipment was moved.

Nuance and implication

Central nuance: Here the subject itself changes direction rather than causing another thing to change direction.

Usual implication: The change often happens because the moving object strikes a surface or encounters another force.

Grammar notes

How to use it
In this meaning, the moving object itself is the subject, so the verb does not need an object.
What can follow it
It is often followed by off and the surface that was hit, or by a word showing the new direction.
Common preposition
Use deflect off something when the object changes direction after contact, as in The ball deflected off the wall.

Useful patterns

deflect off + surface or object

Use this when something changes direction after hitting another object.

Example: The ball deflected off the goalkeeper.

deflect to + direction

Use this to state the direction in which something turns.

Example: The stream deflected to the left.

be deflected by + force or object

Use the passive form when emphasizing what caused the change in direction.

Example: The particles were deflected by the magnetic field.

Common wrong usage

Wrong: The ball deflected from the defender and changed direction.

Correct: The ball deflected off the defender and changed direction.

Use deflect off when something changes direction after hitting another object.

Wrong: The beam was deflected from the magnetic field.

Correct: The beam was deflected by the magnetic field.

Use by to identify the force or object that causes the deflection.

Meaning 3

Redirect attention or criticism

transitive verb /dɪˈflɛkt/ neutral to formal; common in politics, journalism, business, and discussions of communication
🔊 Hear transitive verb pronunciation

to turn attention, criticism, blame, or a difficult question away from a person or subject

In simpler words: turn attention or blame away

Typical pattern: deflect + criticism, blame, attention, question, or responsibility

Natural examples

  1. The spokesperson tried to deflect criticism by emphasizing the company's recent achievements.
  2. The minister deflected questions about the failed project and discussed future plans instead.
  3. Some leaders attempt to deflect blame onto other departments when problems arise.
  4. The company tried to deflect attention away from the environmental impact of the project.
  5. Rather than answering directly, he deflected the question with a joke.
  6. Officials accused the organization of trying to deflect responsibility for the accident.
  7. The advertisement was intended to deflect public attention from the recent controversy.

Nuance and implication

Central nuance: This figurative use treats attention, blame, or criticism as if it were something moving toward a person and being turned elsewhere.

Usual implication: It often suggests avoiding responsibility, an uncomfortable topic, or a direct answer.

Compared with “address”: To address criticism or a question is to deal with it directly. To deflect it is to redirect attention away from it.

Grammar notes

How to use it
This verb normally needs the criticism, blame, attention, question, or responsibility after it.
What can follow it
Common objects include criticism, blame, attention, questions, responsibility, suspicion, and pressure.
Common preposition
Use away from to show what attention is being redirected from. With blame, onto is common when naming the new target, as in deflect blame onto someone else.
Position in a sentence
It is often followed by by and an -ing form to explain how the deflection happens.

Useful patterns

deflect criticism

Use this when someone tries to reduce or redirect criticism.

Example: The company released new figures in an attempt to deflect criticism.

deflect attention away from + noun

Use this when someone tries to make people focus on something else.

Example: The announcement deflected attention away from the budget problems.

deflect blame onto + person or group

Use this when someone tries to make another person appear responsible.

Example: The manager tried to deflect blame onto junior employees.

deflect a question

Use this when someone avoids answering a question directly.

Example: The politician deflected the question and changed the subject.

deflect something by + -ing form

Use this to explain the method used to redirect criticism or attention.

Example: The company tried to deflect criticism by announcing a new environmental program.

Common wrong usage

Wrong: The minister deflected from the question.

Correct: The minister deflected the question.

When deflect means avoid or redirect a question, place the question directly after the verb.

Wrong: The company deflected blame to its suppliers.

Correct: The company deflected blame onto its suppliers.

Use onto when blame is redirected toward another person or group.

Wrong: She deflected attention from herself to not answer.

Correct: She deflected attention away from herself to avoid answering.

Use away from for the original focus and to avoid when explaining the purpose.

countable or uncountable noun

deflection

/dɪˈflɛkʃən/

a change in direction, or an attempt to redirect attention, criticism, or responsibility

Relationship: This noun names the physical change in direction or the figurative act of redirecting attention or criticism.

Pattern: the deflection of + object or a deflection from + issue

Usage note: It is common in physics and engineering, and it is also used figuratively in discussions of communication.

  1. The magnetic field caused a slight deflection of the particles.
  2. Critics described his response as a deflection rather than a direct answer.
  3. Engineers measured the deflection of the beam under pressure.

countable noun

deflector

/dɪˈflɛktər/

a device or surface designed to change the direction of something such as air, heat, water, or particles

Relationship: A deflector is something designed to deflect a moving substance or force.

Pattern: air, wind, heat, or water + deflector

Usage note: It is mainly used in technical and engineering contexts.

  1. The machine uses a metal deflector to direct hot air away from the motor.
  2. A wind deflector was installed above the entrance.

Meaning relations

Synonyms and antonyms

Only clear and useful relationships are included.

Close synonyms

redirect

transitive verb · all senses

Shared meaning: Both verbs can mean cause something to move or focus in a different direction.

Difference: Redirect is a broad, neutral word for sending something in a different direction. Deflect often suggests that the change happens because of contact, force, resistance, or an attempt to avoid something.

Example: The system redirects excess water into a storage tank.

divert

transitive verb · all senses

Shared meaning: Both verbs can describe changing direction or shifting attention.

Difference: Divert often means deliberately send something along a different route or shift resources or attention elsewhere. Deflect often suggests turning something aside from its original path or target.

Example: Traffic was diverted around the damaged bridge.

avoid

transitive verb · Redirect attention or criticism

Shared meaning: Both can describe not dealing directly with an uncomfortable issue.

Difference: Avoid means stay away from or not deal with something. Deflect is more specific and suggests redirecting the question, criticism, or attention elsewhere.

Example: The official avoided answering the most difficult question.

Direct or useful antonyms

address

transitive verb · Redirect attention or criticism

Opposition: To address a question or criticism is to deal with it directly, while to deflect it is to redirect attention away from it.

Example: The minister directly addressed concerns about the new policy.

Precision

Words learners may confuse

reflect

verb

Why it is confusing: Both verbs can describe a change in the path of energy or moving particles.

Key difference: Deflect means change the direction of something. Reflect can mean send light, sound, or heat back from a surface, often toward the side it came from.

deflect: The shield deflects charged particles away from the spacecraft.

reflect: The mirror reflects light back toward the observer.

distract

transitive verb

Why it is confusing: Both can involve changing what someone is paying attention to.

Key difference: Deflect attention means deliberately redirect attention away from a subject. Distract means make it difficult for someone to concentrate, often without giving the attention a specific new target.

deflect: The spokesperson tried to deflect attention from the scandal.

distract: Noise from the hallway distracted the students during the test.
