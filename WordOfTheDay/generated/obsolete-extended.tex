\section{Meanings and usage}
\sectionrule
\subsection{Meaning 1: No longer used or useful}
\textbf{Part of speech:} adjective

\textbf{IPA:} /ˌɑːbsəˈliːt/

\textbf{Pronunciation phrase:} obsolete

\textbf{Local audio file:} audios/obsolete.mp3

\textbf{Register:} neutral to formal; common in technology, business, science, industry, history, and discussions of social change

\textbf{Definition:} no longer used, needed, or useful, usually because something newer or more effective has replaced it

\textbf{In simpler words:} old and no longer useful

\textbf{Typical pattern:} become, make, or render + something + obsolete

\subsubsection{Natural examples}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Many older computer systems became obsolete as faster and cheaper technology was developed.
  \item The company replaced its obsolete equipment with modern automated machines.
  \item Some traditional skills become obsolete when new technologies change the way work is performed.
  \item The invention of digital photography made some types of photographic equipment obsolete.
  \item Scientists abandoned the obsolete classification system after new genetic evidence became available.
  \item The factory continued to rely on obsolete machinery that was expensive to maintain.
  \item Changes in transportation gradually made the old trade route obsolete.
  \item Online databases have rendered many older methods of storing and retrieving information obsolete.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Nuance and implication}
\textbf{Central nuance:} Obsolete does not simply mean old. Something is obsolete because it is no longer useful, necessary, or commonly used under current conditions.

\textbf{Usual implication:} The thing has often been replaced by a newer technology, method, system, or practice.

\textbf{Compared with outdated:} Outdated means old-fashioned or no longer fully suitable for current conditions. Obsolete is stronger and usually means something is no longer used or useful at all.

\subsubsection{Grammar notes}
\textbf{How to use it:} Use it before a noun, as in obsolete equipment, or after a linking verb, as in The equipment is obsolete.

\textbf{What can follow it:} It commonly describes technology, equipment, machinery, methods, systems, skills, ideas, laws, and practices.

\textbf{Position in a sentence:} It often appears after become or be rendered when describing a change over time.

\subsubsection{Useful patterns}
\textbf{Pattern:} become obsolete

\textbf{Use:} Use this when something gradually stops being useful or commonly used.

\textbf{Example:} Many specialized devices became obsolete after smartphones became widely available.

\textbf{Pattern:} make + object + obsolete

\textbf{Use:} Use this when a new development causes an older thing to lose its usefulness.

\textbf{Example:} The new manufacturing process could make several older machines obsolete.

\textbf{Pattern:} render + object + obsolete

\textbf{Use:} This is a more formal way to say that something causes another thing to become obsolete.

\textbf{Example:} Advances in navigation technology rendered the old system obsolete.

\textbf{Pattern:} obsolete + technology, equipment, or machinery

\textbf{Use:} Use this for physical technology that has been replaced by newer alternatives.

\textbf{Example:} The factory still operates with obsolete machinery.

\textbf{Pattern:} obsolete + method, system, or practice

\textbf{Use:} Use this for a way of doing something that is no longer useful or appropriate.

\textbf{Example:} The researchers rejected the obsolete method in favor of a more accurate technique.

\subsubsection{Common wrong usage}
\paragraph{Correction 1}
\textbf{Wrong:} The computer is obsolete because it is five years old.

\textbf{Correct:} The computer may be outdated because it is five years old, but it is obsolete only if it is no longer useful or supported.

\textbf{Why:} Old does not automatically mean obsolete. Obsolete strongly suggests that something is no longer useful or commonly used.

\paragraph{Correction 2}
\textbf{Wrong:} The new technology became the old machines obsolete.

\textbf{Correct:} The new technology made the old machines obsolete.

\textbf{Why:} Use make something obsolete, not become something obsolete.

\paragraph{Correction 3}
\textbf{Wrong:} The old system was obsoleted by the new one.

\textbf{Correct:} The old system was made obsolete by the new one.

\textbf{Why:} For learners, make something obsolete or render something obsolete is much more natural than using obsolete as a verb.

\paragraph{Correction 4}
\textbf{Wrong:} The equipment is very obsoletely.

\textbf{Correct:} The equipment is very obsolete.

\textbf{Why:} Use the adjective obsolete after is.

\section{Word family}
\sectionrule
\subsection{obsolescence}
\textbf{Part of speech:} uncountable noun

\textbf{IPA:} /ˌɑːbsəˈlɛsəns/

\textbf{Definition:} the process or condition of becoming no longer useful or used because something newer has replaced it

\textbf{Relationship to the headword:} This noun names the process or state of becoming obsolete.

\textbf{Grammar pattern:} the obsolescence of + technology or equipment, or technological obsolescence

\textbf{Usage note:} It is formal and normally used without a or an.

\subsubsection{Examples}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Rapid technological change has increased the rate of obsolescence in the electronics industry.
  \item Companies must consider equipment obsolescence when planning long-term investments.
  \item The rapid obsolescence of digital devices creates large amounts of electronic waste.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{obsolescent}
\textbf{Part of speech:} adjective

\textbf{IPA:} /ˌɑːbsəˈlɛsənt/

\textbf{Definition:} becoming obsolete but not yet completely out of use

\textbf{Relationship to the headword:} Obsolescent describes something that is in the process of becoming obsolete.

\textbf{Grammar pattern:} obsolescent + technology, equipment, system, or practice

\textbf{Usage note:} It is considerably less common than obsolete and is mainly used in formal or technical writing.

\subsubsection{Examples}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The company gradually replaced its obsolescent computer systems.
  \item Some obsolescent technologies remain in use because replacing them is expensive.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Common collocations}
\sectionrule
\subsection{become obsolete}
\textbf{Applies to:} No longer used or useful

\textbf{Meaning:} stop being useful or commonly used

\textbf{Example:} Printed telephone directories became obsolete as people began searching for contact information online.

\subsection{make something obsolete}
\textbf{Applies to:} No longer used or useful

\textbf{Meaning:} cause something to stop being useful or necessary

\textbf{Pattern:} make + object + obsolete

\textbf{Example:} The introduction of digital maps made many paper navigation tools obsolete.

\subsection{render something obsolete}
\textbf{Applies to:} No longer used or useful

\textbf{Meaning:} cause something to become no longer useful or relevant

\textbf{Pattern:} render + object + obsolete

\textbf{Example:} The discovery rendered the earlier theory obsolete.

\textbf{Usage note:} Render obsolete is more formal than make obsolete.

\subsection{obsolete technology}
\textbf{Applies to:} No longer used or useful

\textbf{Meaning:} technology that has been replaced by newer alternatives

\textbf{Example:} The hospital replaced obsolete technology with newer diagnostic equipment.

\subsection{obsolete equipment}
\textbf{Applies to:} No longer used or useful

\textbf{Meaning:} equipment that is no longer suitable for current use

\textbf{Example:} Maintaining obsolete equipment can be expensive because replacement parts are difficult to find.

\subsection{obsolete machinery}
\textbf{Applies to:} No longer used or useful

\textbf{Meaning:} old machines that have been replaced by more effective technology

\textbf{Example:} The factory's obsolete machinery limited its productivity.

\subsection{obsolete system}
\textbf{Applies to:} No longer used or useful

\textbf{Meaning:} a system that no longer meets current needs

\textbf{Example:} The government is replacing an obsolete system for storing public records.

\subsection{obsolete method}
\textbf{Applies to:} No longer used or useful

\textbf{Meaning:} a method that has been replaced by a newer or better one

\textbf{Example:} The laboratory stopped using the obsolete method after a more accurate test became available.

\subsection{obsolete skills}
\textbf{Applies to:} No longer used or useful

\textbf{Meaning:} skills that are no longer needed because work practices or technologies have changed

\textbf{Example:} Automation can make some occupational skills obsolete while creating demand for new ones.

\subsection{technological obsolescence}
\textbf{Applies to:} No longer used or useful

\textbf{Meaning:} the process by which technology becomes outdated and no longer useful

\textbf{Example:} Rapid technological obsolescence encourages consumers to replace electronic devices frequently.

\textbf{Usage note:} Obsolescence is the noun form.

\subsection{planned obsolescence}
\textbf{Applies to:} No longer used or useful

\textbf{Meaning:} a business practice in which products are designed or marketed so that consumers will replace them after a limited period

\textbf{Example:} Critics of planned obsolescence argue that products should be designed to last longer.

\textbf{Usage note:} This is an established business and economics term using the noun obsolescence.

\section{Synonyms and antonyms}
\sectionrule
\subsection{Close synonyms}
\subsubsection{outdated}
\textbf{Part of speech:} adjective

\textbf{Applies to:} No longer used or useful

\textbf{Shared meaning:} Both describe something that has fallen behind current developments.

\textbf{Difference:} Outdated means old-fashioned or no longer suitable for current conditions. Something outdated may still be used, while obsolete more strongly suggests that it has been replaced and is no longer useful or necessary.

\textbf{Example:} The textbook contains outdated information about computer technology.

\subsubsection{outmoded}
\textbf{Part of speech:} adjective

\textbf{Applies to:} No longer used or useful

\textbf{Shared meaning:} Both can describe methods, ideas, or systems that have been replaced by newer alternatives.

\textbf{Difference:} Outmoded means no longer fashionable or suitable because newer ideas or methods exist. Obsolete more strongly emphasizes that something has effectively fallen out of use.

\textbf{Example:} The company abandoned its outmoded management practices.

\subsubsection{superseded}
\textbf{Part of speech:} adjective

\textbf{Applies to:} No longer used or useful

\textbf{Shared meaning:} Both can describe something that has been displaced by a newer alternative.

\textbf{Difference:} Superseded specifically means replaced by something newer or more effective. Obsolete describes the resulting condition of no longer being useful or commonly used.

\textbf{Example:} The old standard has been superseded by a newer international standard.

\subsection{Direct or useful antonyms}
\subsubsection{current}
\textbf{Part of speech:} adjective

\textbf{Applies to:} No longer used or useful

\textbf{Opposition:} Current describes something presently used, accepted, or up to date, while obsolete describes something no longer useful or used.

\textbf{Example:} The website provides information about current safety regulations.

\subsubsection{state-of-the-art}
\textbf{Part of speech:} adjective

\textbf{Applies to:} No longer used or useful

\textbf{Opposition:} State-of-the-art describes technology or equipment using the most advanced available methods, while obsolete technology has been replaced by newer alternatives.

\textbf{Usage note:} This opposite is especially useful when talking about technology or equipment.

\textbf{Example:} The laboratory contains state-of-the-art imaging equipment.

\section{Words learners may confuse}
\sectionrule
\subsection{outdated}
\textbf{Part of speech:} adjective

\textbf{Why learners confuse them:} Both describe things that have fallen behind newer developments.

\textbf{Key difference:} Outdated means no longer modern or fully suitable. Obsolete is usually stronger and means no longer useful, necessary, or commonly used because something newer has replaced it.

\textbf{obsolete:} The company can no longer obtain parts for its obsolete computers.

\textbf{outdated:} The website looks outdated, but it still works properly.

\subsection{obsolescent}
\textbf{Part of speech:} adjective

\textbf{Why learners confuse them:} The words belong to the same family but describe different stages of the process.

\textbf{Key difference:} Obsolete means already no longer useful or commonly used. Obsolescent means becoming obsolete but not yet completely out of use.

\textbf{obsolete:} The obsolete system was finally removed.

\textbf{obsolescent:} The obsolescent system is still operating but will soon be replaced.

\section{Etymology}
\sectionrule
\textbf{Origin:} Obsolete comes from Latin obsolescere, meaning to wear out, fall out of use, or become old.

\textbf{Memory link:} Something obsolete has effectively worn out its usefulness because newer conditions or alternatives have replaced it.

\section{Practice exercises}
\sectionrule
\subsection{Word family choice}
Choose the best member of the word family for each blank.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Rapid technological \_\_\_\_\_ can force companies to replace expensive equipment frequently.
  \item The company is gradually replacing its \_\_\_\_\_ computer systems before they become completely obsolete.
  \item The old communication system became \_\_\_\_\_ after the introduction of mobile networks.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Correct or incorrect?}
Decide whether each sentence is correct. Correct the inaccurate ones.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The invention of digital cameras made some older photographic technologies obsolete.
  \item The machine is obsolete simply because it is ten years old.
  \item The new software rendered the old system obsolete.
  \item The new technology became the old equipment obsolete.
  \item The factory still relies on obsolete machinery that is difficult to repair.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Collocation practice}
Complete each sentence with a natural collocation.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Rapid advances in technology can make older devices \_\_\_\_\_.
  \item The factory replaced its obsolete \_\_\_\_\_ with newer automated machines.
  \item The new discovery rendered the previous classification system \_\_\_\_\_.
  \item Rapid technological \_\_\_\_\_ is a major concern for manufacturers of electronic devices.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Complete answer key}
\subsubsection{Word family choice}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{obsolescence.} Rapid technological obsolescence can force companies to replace expensive equipment frequently. \emph{Use the noun obsolescence for the process of becoming obsolete.}
  \item \textbf{obsolescent.} The company is gradually replacing its obsolescent computer systems before they become completely obsolete. \emph{Obsolescent means in the process of becoming obsolete.}
  \item \textbf{obsolete.} The old communication system became obsolete after the introduction of mobile networks. \emph{Use the adjective obsolete after became.}
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Correct or incorrect?}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Correct} \emph{Make something obsolete is a standard pattern.}
  \item \textbf{Incorrect}. The machine may be outdated because it is ten years old, but it is obsolete only if it is no longer useful or has been replaced. \emph{Age alone does not make something obsolete.}
  \item \textbf{Correct} \emph{Render something obsolete is a common formal pattern meaning cause it to become no longer useful.}
  \item \textbf{Incorrect}. The new technology made the old equipment obsolete. \emph{Use make something obsolete, not become something obsolete.}
  \item \textbf{Correct} \emph{Obsolete machinery naturally describes machines that have been replaced by newer technology but may still remain in use.}
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Collocation practice}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{obsolete.} Rapid advances in technology can make older devices obsolete. \emph{Make something obsolete means cause it to become no longer useful or necessary.}
  \item \textbf{machinery.} The factory replaced its obsolete machinery with newer automated machines. \emph{Obsolete machinery is a common expression for equipment that has been replaced by newer technology.}
  \item \textbf{obsolete.} The new discovery rendered the previous classification system obsolete. \emph{Render something obsolete is a formal way to say that something causes it to become no longer useful.}
  \item \textbf{obsolescence.} Rapid technological obsolescence is a major concern for manufacturers of electronic devices. \emph{Technological obsolescence is the process by which technology becomes outdated and loses its usefulness.}
\end{enumerate}
