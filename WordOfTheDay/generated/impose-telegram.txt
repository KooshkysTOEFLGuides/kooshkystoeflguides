🔹 Word: impose
🔊 Primary pronunciation: /ɪmˈpoʊz/

🧠 Meaning 1
Part of speech: transitive verb
Pronunciation: /ɪmˈpoʊz/
Definition: to officially introduce a rule, tax, punishment, restriction, or other requirement and make people obey or accept it

Examples:

1. The government imposed stricter limits on industrial pollution.
2. The court imposed a substantial fine on the company.
3. Several countries imposed new taxes on imported goods.

🧠 Meaning 2
Part of speech: transitive verb
Pronunciation: /ɪmˈpoʊz/
Definition: to force an unwanted burden, idea, condition, or responsibility on someone

Examples:

1. Parents should be careful not to impose their own career ambitions on their children.
2. The new reporting requirements impose a significant burden on small businesses.
3. Critics argued that the policy imposed unnecessary costs on local communities.

🧠 Meaning 3
Part of speech: transitive verb
Pronunciation: /ɪmˈpoʊz/
Definition: to force your presence, attention, or demands on someone when they may not want them

Examples:

1. I did not want to impose on my neighbors by asking them to drive me to the airport.
2. She felt that staying for another week would impose on her hosts.
3. He was careful not to impose himself on colleagues who were already extremely busy.
