Meanings and usage

The core definition and examples are always included. Additional notes appear only when they are accurate and useful.

Meaning 1

Correct and appropriate behavior

uncountable noun /prəˈpraɪəti/ formal; common in legal, ethical, professional, political, and institutional contexts
🔊 Hear uncountable noun pronunciation

behavior that is considered morally, socially, or professionally correct and appropriate

In simpler words: being correct and appropriate

Typical pattern: the propriety of + action or decision

Natural examples

  1. The committee questioned the propriety of allowing the director to approve a contract involving his own company.
  2. Employees are expected to observe professional propriety when dealing with confidential information.
  3. The debate focused on the propriety of using public funds for the project.
  4. Several scholars raised concerns about the ethical propriety of conducting the experiment without full consent.
  5. The judge considered whether the official's actions met accepted standards of propriety.
  6. Critics questioned the propriety of giving government contracts to close political allies.
  7. The organization introduced new rules to ensure propriety in its financial decisions.
  8. Questions about the propriety of the procedure delayed the final decision.

Nuance and implication

Central nuance: Propriety is about whether behavior or a decision fits accepted standards of what is proper, appropriate, ethical, or respectable.

Usual implication: The word often appears when people are evaluating whether an action crosses a moral, professional, or procedural boundary.

Compared with “impropriety”: Propriety means behavior that meets accepted standards, while impropriety means behavior that violates those standards.

Grammar notes

How to use it
In this meaning, propriety is normally uncountable. It often appears in formal expressions such as the propriety of an action or questions about propriety.
What can follow it
It is commonly followed by of and then the action, decision, or behavior being evaluated.
Common preposition
Use the propriety of + noun or -ing form, as in the propriety of the decision or the propriety of accepting the gift.
Noun use
Do not normally use a or an before propriety in this meaning.
Position in a sentence
It often appears after words such as question, doubt, consider, discuss, ensure, or observe.

Useful patterns

the propriety of + noun or -ing form

Use this when evaluating whether an action or decision is appropriate.

Example: The board debated the propriety of accepting donations from the company.

question the propriety of + action

Use this when expressing doubt about whether something is proper or acceptable.

Example: Several members questioned the propriety of holding the vote in private.

standards of propriety

Use this for accepted rules about proper conduct.

Example: Public officials are expected to follow high standards of propriety.

professional propriety

Use this for behavior that meets accepted professional standards.

Example: The investigation examined whether the lawyer had maintained professional propriety.

ethical propriety

Use this when judging whether an action is ethically acceptable.

Example: The researchers debated the ethical propriety of the proposed experiment.

Common wrong usage

Wrong: The committee discussed about the propriety of the decision.

Correct: The committee discussed the propriety of the decision.

Discuss normally takes its object directly, without about.

Wrong: They questioned a propriety of using public funds.

Correct: They questioned the propriety of using public funds.

In this meaning, propriety is normally uncountable and commonly appears as the propriety of.

Wrong: Propriety means that something is legal.

Correct: Propriety means that something is considered appropriate or proper.

An action can be legal but still be considered improper. Propriety is broader than legality.

Wrong: The official acted with many proprieties.

Correct: The official acted with propriety.

When referring generally to proper conduct, use the uncountable noun propriety.

Meaning 2

Accepted rules of polite behavior

plural countable noun /prəˈpraɪəti/ formal and somewhat old-fashioned; mainly used in discussions of etiquette, social customs, literature, and history
🔊 Hear plural countable noun pronunciation

the accepted rules of polite and socially appropriate behavior

In simpler words: rules of proper social behavior

Typical pattern: observe, follow, ignore, or respect + the proprieties

Natural examples

  1. Guests were expected to observe the proprieties of formal diplomatic events.
  2. He cared little about social proprieties and often spoke very directly.
  3. The novel shows how strictly the proprieties of upper-class society controlled personal behavior.
  4. She followed all the usual proprieties when meeting the ambassador.
  5. Changes in social attitudes have weakened some of the proprieties that governed public behavior in the past.
  6. The ceremony was conducted according to the traditional proprieties of the community.

Nuance and implication

Central nuance: The plural proprieties refers to specific conventions about how people are expected to behave politely or respectably.

Usual implication: These rules are usually created by social tradition rather than formal law.

Compared with “breach of etiquette”: Observing the proprieties means following accepted social conventions, while a breach of etiquette means violating one of them.

Grammar notes

How to use it
This meaning is normally plural and commonly appears as the proprieties or social proprieties.
What can follow it
It often appears after verbs such as observe, follow, respect, maintain, or ignore.
Noun use
Use the plural proprieties when referring to particular social conventions or rules of polite behavior.
Position in a sentence
The expression observe the proprieties is especially common in formal writing.

Useful patterns

observe the proprieties

Use this when someone follows accepted rules of polite or respectable behavior.

Example: Despite their disagreement, both diplomats carefully observed the proprieties.

social proprieties

Use this for accepted social conventions about correct behavior.

Example: The novel frequently challenges the social proprieties of the period.

ignore the proprieties

Use this when someone does not follow expected social conventions.

Example: He ignored the proprieties and openly criticized his host during dinner.

Common wrong usage

Wrong: She followed the propriety of formal dinners.

Correct: She followed the proprieties of formal dinners.

When referring to specific rules of social behavior, the plural proprieties is normally used.

Wrong: Social proprieties are official laws.

Correct: Social proprieties are accepted conventions of proper behavior.

Proprieties are social expectations, not necessarily legal rules.

Relationship: Proper expresses the basic idea of being correct or appropriate that is contained in propriety.

Pattern: proper + behavior, procedure, method, care, or use

countable or uncountable noun

impropriety

/ˌɪmprəˈpraɪəti/

Relationship: Impropriety is the opposite form of propriety and refers to behavior that violates accepted standards.

Pattern: financial, ethical, professional, or procedural + impropriety

Usage note: The plural improprieties is possible when referring to separate improper acts.

  1. The investigation found no evidence of financial impropriety.
  2. The official resigned after allegations of serious impropriety.
  3. Several procedural improprieties were identified during the review.

Meaning relations

Synonyms and antonyms

Only clear and useful relationships are included.

Close synonyms

appropriateness

uncountable noun · Correct and appropriate behavior

Shared meaning: Both can refer to whether something is suitable or proper.

Difference: Appropriateness is the general quality of being suitable for a situation. Propriety is more formal and often carries moral, professional, or social judgment.

Example: The committee considered the appropriateness of using the material in a classroom setting.

decorum

uncountable noun · Accepted rules of polite behavior

Shared meaning: Both can refer to behavior that follows accepted social standards.

Difference: Decorum emphasizes polite, dignified behavior, especially in formal situations. Propriety is broader and can refer to social, ethical, or professional correctness.

Example: Members of the audience were expected to maintain decorum during the ceremony.

correctness

uncountable noun · Correct and appropriate behavior

Shared meaning: Both can express the idea of meeting an accepted standard.

Difference: Correctness is broader and can refer to factual, grammatical, or procedural accuracy. Propriety specifically concerns whether conduct or a decision is proper or acceptable.

Example: The researchers checked the correctness of the calculations.

Direct or useful antonyms

impropriety

countable or uncountable noun · Correct and appropriate behavior

Opposition: Propriety means proper or appropriate conduct, while impropriety means conduct that violates accepted moral, professional, or social standards.

Example: The investigation found evidence of serious financial impropriety.

indecorum

uncountable noun · Accepted rules of polite behavior

Opposition: Indecorum means behavior that violates accepted standards of politeness or dignity, while observing the proprieties means following those standards.

Usage note: Indecorum is formal and relatively uncommon.

Example: His behavior at the formal ceremony was criticized as indecorum.

Precision

Words learners may confuse

property

noun

Why it is confusing: The words are spelled and pronounced somewhat similarly but have very different modern meanings.

Key difference: Propriety means proper or acceptable behavior. Property means something owned by a person or organization, or a characteristic of a substance or object.

propriety: The committee questioned the propriety of the arrangement.

property: The company owns property in several cities.

proprietary

noun versus adjective

Why it is confusing: The words share a similar form and historical connection with ideas of what belongs or is proper.

Key difference: Propriety concerns proper or acceptable conduct. Proprietary means relating to ownership or to something legally owned and controlled by a person or company.

propriety: There were doubts about the propriety of the official's behavior.

proprietary: The company refused to reveal its proprietary technology.

impropriety

noun

Why it is confusing: Impropriety is formed by adding the negative prefix im- to propriety.

Key difference: Propriety means appropriate or acceptable conduct. Impropriety means conduct that is inappropriate, unethical, or unacceptable.

propriety: The official acted with complete propriety.

impropriety: The audit uncovered evidence of financial impropriety.

Word history

Etymology

Only confidently documented details are shown.

Origin

Propriety comes through French from Latin proprietas, which referred to ownership, a particular character, or what properly belongs to something.

Development

The word gradually developed the sense of what is proper or appropriate, especially in behavior and social conduct.

Memory link

Think of propriety as behaving in the way that is considered proper.
