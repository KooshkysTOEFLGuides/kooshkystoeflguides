Meanings and usage

The core definition and examples are always included. Additional notes appear only when they are accurate and useful.

Meaning 1

Serious confusion or disorder

uncountable noun /ˈtɝːmɔɪl/ neutral to formal; common in news, history, politics, economics, business, and academic writing
🔊 Hear uncountable noun pronunciation

a state of great confusion, uncertainty, or disorder, especially during a period of serious change or conflict

In simpler words: serious confusion and disorder

Typical pattern: in turmoil; throw or plunge something into turmoil; political or economic turmoil

Natural examples

  1. The country experienced years of political turmoil after the government collapsed.
  2. Financial turmoil caused investors to become much more cautious.
  3. The sudden leadership change threw the organization into turmoil.
  4. The region remained in turmoil following months of violent conflict.
  5. Economic turmoil led many businesses to postpone major investments.
  6. The unexpected announcement created considerable turmoil within the university.
  7. The industry went through a period of turmoil as new technologies disrupted traditional business models.
  8. Social turmoil often increases when people lose confidence in public institutions.

Nuance and implication

Central nuance: Turmoil suggests more than ordinary confusion. It usually describes a serious and unstable situation in which normal order has been disrupted.

Usual implication: The situation is often changing rapidly, difficult to control, and stressful for the people or institutions involved.

Compared with “stability”: Stability means conditions are relatively steady and predictable. Turmoil means conditions are highly unsettled, confused, or disorderly.

Grammar notes

How to use it
Turmoil is normally used as an uncountable noun, so we usually do not say a turmoil or turmoils.
What can follow it
It often appears after in, into, amid, or during. It is also frequently modified by words such as political, economic, financial, social, or emotional.
Common preposition
Use in turmoil for a state of disorder and into turmoil after verbs such as throw or plunge.
Noun use
Turmoil is usually uncountable. Say political turmoil, a period of turmoil, or the country was in turmoil rather than a turmoil.
Position in a sentence
It commonly appears after be or remain in, and after verbs such as cause, create, throw, or plunge.

Useful patterns

be in turmoil

Use this when a country, organization, market, or other system is experiencing serious disorder or uncertainty.

Example: The company was in turmoil after several senior executives resigned.

throw + something + into turmoil

Use this when an event suddenly creates serious confusion or instability.

Example: The unexpected court ruling threw the industry into turmoil.

plunge + something + into turmoil

Use this for a sudden and serious move into disorder or instability.

Example: The political crisis plunged the country into turmoil.

political or economic turmoil

Use these common combinations to describe instability in government or the economy.

Example: Years of economic turmoil reduced public confidence in financial institutions.

amid or during turmoil

Use this when something happens while serious disorder or uncertainty is continuing.

Example: Several companies failed amid the financial turmoil.

Common wrong usage

Wrong: The country experienced a turmoil after the election.

Correct: The country experienced turmoil after the election.

Turmoil is normally uncountable, so do not usually use a before it.

Wrong: The company was on turmoil after the director resigned.

Correct: The company was in turmoil after the director resigned.

The standard expression is in turmoil.

Wrong: The announcement threw the company in turmoil.

Correct: The announcement threw the company into turmoil.

Use into turmoil after throw or plunge.

Wrong: There were several turmoils in the economy.

Correct: There were several periods of turmoil in the economy.

Turmoil is normally uncountable, so use periods of turmoil rather than turmoils.

tumultuous

/tuːˈmʌltʃuəs/

full of confusion, disorder, strong emotion, or rapid change

Relationship: Tumultuous is not a direct morphological form of turmoil, but it is a closely related academic adjective often used for situations characterized by turmoil.

Pattern: tumultuous + period, year, history, relationship, or change

Usage note: Use tumultuous to describe a period or situation; use turmoil as the noun for the state of disorder itself.

  1. The country went through a tumultuous period of political change.
  2. The company had a tumultuous year marked by resignations and financial losses.
  3. The reform was introduced during a tumultuous period in the nation's history.

Meaning relations

Synonyms and antonyms

Only clear and useful relationships are included.

Close synonyms

chaos

uncountable noun · Serious confusion or disorder

Shared meaning: Both can describe serious disorder and confusion.

Difference: Chaos suggests an extreme lack of order or organization. Turmoil often includes instability, conflict, and uncertainty and does not always mean complete disorder.

Example: The sudden evacuation created chaos at the airport.

upheaval

countable or uncountable noun · Serious confusion or disorder

Shared meaning: Both often describe unstable periods of major social, political, or economic change.

Difference: Upheaval emphasizes a major change or disruption to an existing system. Turmoil emphasizes the confusion, uncertainty, and instability that may accompany such change.

Example: Industrialization caused major social upheaval in many communities.

disorder

uncountable noun · Serious confusion or disorder

Shared meaning: Both can describe a situation in which normal order has broken down.

Difference: Disorder is broader and simply means lack of order. Turmoil usually suggests a more intense, unstable, and emotionally or politically charged situation.

Example: The collapse of local authority led to widespread disorder.

unrest

uncountable noun · Serious confusion or disorder

Shared meaning: Both can describe unstable social or political conditions.

Difference: Unrest usually refers specifically to social or political dissatisfaction that may lead to protests, strikes, or violence. Turmoil is broader and can also describe economic, institutional, or personal instability.

Example: Rising food prices contributed to widespread social unrest.

Direct or useful antonyms

stability

uncountable noun · Serious confusion or disorder

Opposition: Stability is a condition in which things remain relatively steady and predictable, while turmoil involves serious uncertainty, disorder, or instability.

Example: The reforms were intended to restore economic stability.

order

uncountable noun · Serious confusion or disorder

Opposition: Order means that people, systems, or activities are organized and controlled, while turmoil means normal order has been seriously disrupted.

Example: Authorities gradually restored order after the crisis.

Precision

Words learners may confuse

chaos

uncountable noun

Why it is confusing: Both describe serious disorder, but chaos is often more extreme.

Key difference: Chaos usually suggests almost complete disorder. Turmoil can describe serious instability and confusion even when some structure still remains.

turmoil: The company was in turmoil after its chief executive resigned.

chaos: The evacuation descended into chaos when the communication system failed.

unrest

uncountable noun

Why it is confusing: Both are common in news and academic discussions of unstable situations.

Key difference: Unrest usually involves dissatisfaction among a group of people, often expressed through protests or conflict. Turmoil is broader and can describe political, economic, organizational, or emotional instability.

turmoil: The banking system was in turmoil after several institutions collapsed.

unrest: The government faced growing unrest among workers and students.

upheaval

countable or uncountable noun

Why it is confusing: Both frequently appear in descriptions of major political, economic, and social change.

Key difference: Upheaval emphasizes a major disruption or change itself. Turmoil emphasizes the confused and unstable condition that results from or accompanies such disruption.

turmoil: The country remained in turmoil for months after the revolution.

upheaval: The revolution caused a major political upheaval.
