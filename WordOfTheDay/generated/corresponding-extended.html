Meanings and usage

The core definition and examples are always included. Additional notes appear only when they are accurate and useful.

Meaning 1

Directly related or matching

adjective /ˌkɔːrəˈspɑːndɪŋ/ neutral to formal; very common in academic, scientific, technical, and analytical writing
🔊 Hear adjective pronunciation

directly related to something else, especially because the two things change together, belong together, or have matching positions

In simpler words: matching or directly connected

Typical pattern: a corresponding + increase, decrease, change, number, section, or item

Natural examples

  1. An increase in production was accompanied by a corresponding increase in energy use.
  2. Each number on the map refers to a corresponding location in the table.
  3. Higher levels of education are often associated with a corresponding rise in average income.
  4. The decline in rainfall led to a corresponding reduction in crop yields.
  5. As the population grew, there was a corresponding increase in demand for housing.
  6. Each category in the chart has a corresponding symbol on the map.
  7. The researchers observed a corresponding change in behavior after the environmental conditions were altered.
  8. Lower transportation costs produced a corresponding decrease in the final price of the goods.

Nuance and implication

Central nuance: Corresponding emphasizes a direct relationship or match between two things rather than a general similarity.

Usual implication: When one thing changes, appears, or occupies a particular position, the corresponding thing is the one directly connected with it.

Compared with “unrelated”: Corresponding things have a clear relationship or match, while unrelated things have no such direct connection.

Grammar notes

How to use it
Use corresponding before a noun when identifying the thing that directly matches or relates to another thing.
What can follow it
Common nouns include increase, decrease, change, rise, fall, number, section, category, value, and item.
Common preposition
When the other thing must be stated explicitly, corresponding to is common, as in the value corresponding to each category.
Position in a sentence
Corresponding is especially common before nouns, as in a corresponding increase or the corresponding section.

Useful patterns

a corresponding increase or rise

Use this when one increase is directly connected with another.

Example: The expansion of the city produced a corresponding increase in traffic.

a corresponding decrease or reduction

Use this when a decrease is directly related to another change.

Example: Greater efficiency resulted in a corresponding reduction in fuel consumption.

a corresponding change

Use this for a change that matches or results from another change.

Example: The shift in temperature caused a corresponding change in the animals' behavior.

the corresponding + item, number, section, or category

Use this to identify the item that matches another item in a different list, table, diagram, or system.

Example: Match each definition with the corresponding term.

corresponding to + noun

Use this when explicitly identifying what something matches.

Example: The values corresponding to each age group are shown in the final column.

Common wrong usage

Wrong: The population increased, with a correspond increase in housing demand.

Correct: The population increased, with a corresponding increase in housing demand.

Use the adjective corresponding before a noun such as increase.

Wrong: Each number has a corresponding with a location on the map.

Correct: Each number has a corresponding location on the map.

Corresponding can directly modify a noun; with is not needed in this structure.

Wrong: The corresponding increase means the two increases are exactly equal.

Correct: The corresponding increase means the two changes are directly related.

Corresponding does not necessarily mean identical in size. It means directly related or matching in some relevant way.

Meaning 2

Equivalent in another system or group

adjective /ˌkɔːrəˈspɑːndɪŋ/ neutral to formal; common in academic, technical, administrative, and comparative contexts
🔊 Hear adjective pronunciation

having the same or a very similar function, position, or meaning as something in another system, group, or situation

In simpler words: the matching equivalent

Typical pattern: the corresponding + part, section, term, position, value, or item

Natural examples

  1. Researchers compared each region with the corresponding region in the earlier survey.
  2. The French term has no exact corresponding expression in English.
  3. Students should enter each answer in the corresponding box on the form.
  4. Each department sends its report to the corresponding office at the national level.
  5. The two diagrams use different colors to identify corresponding parts of the structure.
  6. The researchers compared the experimental group with the corresponding control group.
  7. Each chapter in the revised edition includes a corresponding section in the online materials.
  8. The numbered samples were placed beside their corresponding labels.

Nuance and implication

Central nuance: This sense emphasizes equivalence of role, position, or meaning across two separate systems, groups, documents, or sets.

Usual implication: The two things are not necessarily identical, but each occupies a comparable place or performs a comparable function.

Compared with “identical”: Corresponding things match in role, position, or relationship, but they do not have to be completely identical.

Grammar notes

How to use it
Use corresponding before the noun that has an equivalent in another group or system.
What can follow it
Common nouns include term, section, position, value, part, office, category, and label.
Common preposition
Corresponding to can identify the thing that provides the match: the English term corresponding to the French expression.
Position in a sentence
The definite article the is common when a unique matching item is intended: the corresponding section.

Useful patterns

the corresponding section or part

Use this when identifying the equivalent part of another document, object, or system.

Example: The same information appears in the corresponding section of the second report.

the corresponding term or expression

Use this when comparing vocabulary or terminology across languages or fields.

Example: There is no precise corresponding term in English.

the corresponding value

Use this for a value that matches a particular variable, category, or position.

Example: Find the temperature and record the corresponding pressure value.

corresponding parts

Use this for parts in separate objects or diagrams that occupy equivalent positions.

Example: The corresponding parts of the two organisms perform similar functions.

Common wrong usage

Wrong: Write your answer in the correspondingly box.

Correct: Write your answer in the corresponding box.

Use the adjective corresponding before the noun box.

Wrong: Corresponding means completely identical.

Correct: Corresponding means matching in position, function, relationship, or meaning.

Corresponding things may differ in details even though they occupy equivalent roles.

Wrong: The English word is corresponding with the French term.

Correct: The English word corresponds to the French term.

When using the verb correspond to describe equivalence, correspond to is usually more natural than be corresponding with.

Morphology

Word family

Only common and educationally useful family members are included.

intransitive verb

correspond

/ˌkɔːrəˈspɑːnd/

to match, agree with, or be closely related to something

Relationship: Corresponding is formed from the verb correspond and describes something that matches or is directly related to another thing.

Pattern: correspond to or correspond with + noun

Usage note: Correspond to is especially common for equivalence or matching. Correspond with can also mean agree with, and it has a separate meaning of exchange letters or messages.

  1. The results correspond closely to the predictions of the model.
  2. Each symbol corresponds to a particular type of material.
  3. The boundaries shown on the map do not correspond exactly with modern political borders.

countable or uncountable noun

correspondence

/ˌkɔːrəˈspɑːndəns/

a close similarity or relationship between two things

Relationship: This noun can name the matching relationship expressed by corresponding.

Pattern: a correspondence between + two things

Usage note: Correspondence also commonly means letters or messages exchanged between people.

  1. Researchers found a close correspondence between the two sets of data.
  2. There is no exact correspondence between the categories used by the two studies.

adverb

correspondingly

/ˌkɔːrəˈspɑːndɪŋli/

in a way that matches or changes in relation to something else

Relationship: This adverb describes an action or change that occurs in a matching or related way.

Pattern: verb + correspondingly, or correspondingly + verb

Usage note: It is formal and less common than the adjective corresponding.

  1. Production costs fell, and prices decreased correspondingly.
  2. As demand increased, the company correspondingly expanded its workforce.

Meaning relations

Synonyms and antonyms

Only clear and useful relationships are included.

Close synonyms

matching

adjective · Equivalent in another system or group

Shared meaning: Both can describe things that belong together or have equivalent roles.

Difference: Matching is the more general and everyday word. Corresponding is more formal and often emphasizes equivalent position, function, or relationship rather than visual similarity.

Example: Place each card beside its matching label.

equivalent

adjective · Equivalent in another system or group

Shared meaning: Both can describe things with comparable roles or meanings.

Difference: Equivalent emphasizes having the same value, function, meaning, or effect. Corresponding emphasizes that two things occupy matching positions or are directly related.

Example: The two qualifications are considered academically equivalent.

related

adjective · Directly related or matching

Shared meaning: Both describe a connection between things.

Difference: Related is much broader and simply means connected in some way. Corresponding suggests a more specific one-to-one or parallel relationship.

Example: The researchers examined several related environmental factors.

parallel

adjective · Directly related or matching

Shared meaning: Both can describe similar or related developments.

Difference: Parallel often describes similar developments occurring side by side. Corresponding more directly identifies one thing as the match or counterpart of another.

Example: The two countries experienced parallel economic changes.

Direct or useful antonyms

unrelated

adjective · Directly related or matching

Opposition: Corresponding things have a direct relationship or match, while unrelated things have no meaningful connection.

Example: The two changes appear to be unrelated.

nonmatching

adjective · Equivalent in another system or group

Opposition: Corresponding items match in position, role, or relationship, while nonmatching items do not.

Usage note: This contrast is useful in technical contexts but is less common than unrelated.

Example: The software highlighted the nonmatching entries in the two datasets.

Precision

Words learners may confuse

correspondent

adjective versus countable noun

Why it is confusing: The words come from the same verb and look very similar.

Key difference: Corresponding is usually an adjective meaning matching or related. A correspondent is normally a person who reports news or exchanges letters or messages with someone.

corresponding: Enter each number in the corresponding box.

correspondent: The newspaper sent a foreign correspondent to report on the conflict.

correspond

adjective versus intransitive verb

Why it is confusing: They express the same basic relationship but have different grammatical roles.

Key difference: Correspond is a verb meaning match, agree, or be equivalent. Corresponding is the adjective used before a noun to describe the matching thing.

corresponding: Each diagram has a corresponding explanation.

correspond: Each diagram corresponds to an explanation in the text.

respective

adjective

Why it is confusing: Both are often used when pairing or distinguishing items in formal writing.

Key difference: Respective means belonging separately to each of two or more people or things already mentioned. Corresponding means matching or occupying an equivalent position.

corresponding: Match each graph with its corresponding description.

respective: The two researchers returned to their respective universities.

Word history

Etymology

Only confidently documented details are shown.

Origin

Corresponding comes from correspond, which ultimately comes from Latin elements meaning to answer together or respond in agreement.

Development

The idea of things answering or matching one another developed into the modern sense of having a direct relationship or equivalent position.

Memory link

If two things correspond, they match each other; the corresponding item is the one that matches.
