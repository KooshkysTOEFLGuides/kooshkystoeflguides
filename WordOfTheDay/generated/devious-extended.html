Meanings and usage

The core definition and examples are always included. Additional notes appear only when they are accurate and useful.

Meaning 1

Dishonest and cleverly indirect

adjective /ˈdiːviəs/ neutral to formal; strongly negative and common in descriptions of dishonest, manipulative, or secretive behavior
🔊 Hear adjective pronunciation

using dishonest, secretive, or clever methods to get what you want

In simpler words: sneaky and dishonest

Typical pattern: devious + tactic, scheme, method, plan, motive, or person

Natural examples

  1. The manager used devious tactics to hide the true cost of the project.
  2. Investigators uncovered a devious scheme to avoid paying taxes.
  3. The character is intelligent but devious and frequently manipulates people for his own benefit.
  4. The company was accused of using devious methods to discourage customers from canceling their subscriptions.
  5. His devious plan depended on keeping several important facts secret.
  6. The politician's opponents described the campaign strategy as devious and misleading.
  7. She suspected that he had a devious motive for offering to help.
  8. A devious negotiator may appear cooperative while secretly trying to gain an unfair advantage.

Nuance and implication

Central nuance: Devious suggests dishonesty combined with cleverness or indirectness rather than simple open wrongdoing.

Usual implication: A devious person often hides their true intentions and uses complicated or manipulative methods to gain an advantage.

Compared with “straightforward”: A straightforward person or method is open, direct, and easy to understand. A devious one achieves its purpose through hidden, indirect, or dishonest means.

Grammar notes

How to use it
Use it before a noun, as in a devious plan, or after a linking verb, as in His methods were devious.
What can follow it
It commonly describes people, tactics, schemes, plans, methods, strategies, and motives.
Position in a sentence
Words such as extremely, highly, particularly, and remarkably can appear before devious.

Useful patterns

a devious plan or scheme

Use this for a dishonest plan involving secrecy or manipulation.

Example: The group developed a devious scheme to obtain confidential information.

devious tactics or methods

Use this for dishonest or manipulative ways of achieving a goal.

Example: The investigation revealed that the firm had used devious tactics to eliminate competitors.

a devious person

Use this for someone who regularly uses dishonest or indirect methods.

Example: He was a devious businessman who rarely revealed his true intentions.

a devious motive

Use this when someone's hidden reason for doing something is dishonest or manipulative.

Example: She wondered whether there was a devious motive behind the generous offer.

Common wrong usage

Wrong: He is devious because he likes solving difficult puzzles.

Correct: He is clever because he likes solving difficult puzzles.

Devious is negative and suggests dishonesty or manipulation, not simply intelligence.

Wrong: The company used deviously tactics.

Correct: The company used devious tactics.

Use the adjective devious before the noun tactics.

Wrong: She made a devious because she wanted to win.

Correct: She made a devious plan because she wanted to win.

Devious is an adjective, so it normally needs a noun in this position.

Wrong: His devious behavior was honest and completely transparent.

Correct: His behavior was honest and completely transparent.

Devious normally implies hidden, dishonest, or manipulative behavior, so it conflicts with honest and transparent.

Meaning 2

Indirect or winding

adjective /ˈdiːviəs/ formal and less common than the dishonest meaning; mainly used for routes, paths, and courses
🔊 Hear adjective pronunciation

indirect and complicated rather than following the simplest or most direct route

In simpler words: indirect and winding

Typical pattern: devious + route, path, course, or way

Natural examples

  1. Because the main road was closed, we had to take a devious route through several small villages.
  2. The river follows a devious course through the mountains.
  3. The hikers reached the valley by a long and devious path.
  4. The old road takes a devious route around the steepest part of the hill.
  5. Explorers followed a devious trail through the dense forest.
  6. The stream takes a devious course before eventually reaching the lake.

Nuance and implication

Central nuance: This sense describes physical movement that does not follow a direct line.

Usual implication: The route may contain many turns or require extra distance.

Compared with “direct”: A direct route follows the shortest or simplest path. A devious route turns away from that direct path.

Grammar notes

How to use it
Use it before nouns such as route, path, course, or way.
What can follow it
It most naturally describes a route or physical course rather than a person.
Position in a sentence
This meaning is less common in everyday English than the dishonest meaning.

Useful patterns

a devious route

Use this for a route that reaches a destination in an indirect way.

Example: The travelers took a devious route around the flooded area.

a devious path

Use this for a path with many turns or changes in direction.

Example: A devious path led through the forest to the old village.

a devious course

Use this for a river, road, or other line of movement that follows an indirect direction.

Example: The river follows a devious course across the plain.

Common wrong usage

Wrong: We took a devious route because it was the shortest and most direct road.

Correct: We took a direct route because it was the shortest road.

A devious route is indirect or winding, not the shortest and most direct route.

Wrong: The road moved devious through the mountains.

Correct: The road followed a devious route through the mountains.

Use the adjective devious before a noun such as route or course.

Meaning relations

Synonyms and antonyms

Only clear and useful relationships are included.

Close synonyms

sly

adjective · Dishonest and cleverly indirect

Shared meaning: Both can describe clever behavior that is not completely open or honest.

Difference: Sly often describes clever behavior that is secretive or slightly dishonest and can sometimes sound playful. Devious is more strongly negative and suggests deliberate manipulation or dishonesty.

Example: He gave a sly smile after secretly changing the plan.

underhanded

adjective · Dishonest and cleverly indirect

Shared meaning: Both describe dishonest methods that are not open or straightforward.

Difference: Underhanded strongly emphasizes unfair or dishonest behavior carried out secretly. Devious also emphasizes cleverness and indirectness.

Example: The company was accused of using underhanded methods to eliminate competition.

cunning

adjective · Dishonest and cleverly indirect

Shared meaning: Both can describe clever strategies that are not obvious.

Difference: Cunning emphasizes skill in achieving goals through clever planning and may be positive or negative depending on context. Devious is normally negative and emphasizes dishonesty or manipulation.

Example: The animal uses a cunning strategy to obtain food.

winding

adjective · Indirect or winding

Shared meaning: Both can describe a route that is not straight.

Difference: Winding simply describes something with many bends or turns. Devious is more formal and emphasizes that the route is indirect.

Example: A winding road climbs through the mountains.

Direct or useful antonyms

straightforward

adjective · Dishonest and cleverly indirect

Opposition: Straightforward behavior is open, honest, and direct, while devious behavior relies on hidden or manipulative methods.

Example: She gave a straightforward explanation of what had happened.

direct

adjective · Indirect or winding

Opposition: A direct route follows the simplest path toward a destination, while a devious route is indirect and winding.

Example: We took the most direct route to the village.

Precision

Words learners may confuse

deviant

adjective

Why it is confusing: The words look similar and both come from roots connected with turning away from a normal path.

Key difference: Devious means dishonest and manipulative or, less commonly, indirect and winding. Deviant describes behavior or a person that differs significantly from accepted social norms.

devious: The executive used devious tactics to hide the company's losses.

deviant: The study examined behavior considered deviant by the surrounding society.

deviate

adjective versus verb

Why it is confusing: The words share a similar form and both contain the idea of moving away from a direct or expected path.

Key difference: Devious is an adjective describing dishonest behavior or an indirect route. Deviate is a verb meaning move away from an expected path, standard, or plan.

devious: They developed a devious plan to avoid the regulations.

deviate: The aircraft had to deviate from its original route because of bad weather.

Word history

Etymology

Only confidently documented details are shown.

Origin

Devious comes from Latin devius, meaning out of the way or away from the road.

Development

The original idea of leaving the direct path developed into the figurative meaning of using indirect, secretive, or dishonest methods.

Memory link

A devious person does not take the straight path toward a goal; they use indirect and often dishonest methods.
