## 1. Education and Learning

**Questions:** RT_002, RT_013, RT_018, RT_020, RT_025, RT_030, RT_031
**7 corpus entries; 6 distinct questions because RT_018 and RT_030 are duplicates**

Recurring areas:

* technology in education
* active participation versus lectures
* teaching methods
* project-based learning
* exams versus alternative assessment
* traditional versus innovative school subjects
* the knowledge and skills schools should prioritise
* preparing students for future social or environmental problems

This should clearly be one major bank.

---

## 2. Technology, Artificial Intelligence, and Automation

**Questions:** RT_003, RT_016, RT_032

Recurring areas:

* benefits and dangers of AI
* job displacement
* privacy and data collection
* automated bias
* surveillance and personal freedom
* automation and productivity
* technology replacing human labour
* psychological and social consequences of technological change

This bank would concern **technology’s effects on individuals, employment, rights, and society**, rather than classroom technology.

---

## 3. Digital Media, Communication, and Relationships

**Questions:** RT_005, RT_008, RT_010, RT_026
**Four entries, although RT_005 and RT_008 are essentially versions of the same question**

Recurring areas:

* digital communication and personal relationships
* online versus face-to-face interaction
* maintaining long-distance relationships
* emotional depth and misunderstanding
* social media and public opinion
* echo chambers
* access to different viewpoints
* misinformation
* mental health
* social connection and isolation

This deserves its own bank because the mechanisms are quite consistent across the questions.

Recurring areas:

* advertising and consumer decisions

## 5. Work, Careers, and Professional Life

**Questions:** RT_009, RT_022, RT_034

Recurring areas:

* salary and job security
* personal fulfilment
* work–life balance
* emotional intelligence
* technical competence
* teamwork and communication
* workplace performance
* stress and burnout
* maintaining relationships while working full-time

This could theoretically be placed inside business, but I would keep it separate because the questions concern **individual working life**, not company decisions.

## 6. Society, Culture, Identity, and Human Behaviour

**Questions:** RT_001, RT_011, RT_012, RT_015, RT_035

Recurring areas:

* nature versus nurture
* cultural globalisation
* preservation of local traditions
* indigenous cultures
* adaptation versus cultural preservation
* social norms
* conformity and individual freedom
* art as a reflection of society
* art as a cause of social change
* identity, behaviour, and cultural diversity

This bank would cover the broad human and social questions that do not fit naturally into education, technology, or business.

---

## 7. Government, Economics, History, and International Institutions

**Questions:** RT_004, RT_019, RT_023

Recurring areas:

* individuals versus large historical forces
* government regulation versus economic freedom
* stability and consumer protection
* innovation and efficiency
* international organisations
* global cooperation
* enforcement and institutional reform
* leadership, institutions, and structural forces

These questions are not identical, but they share a central concern:

> **Who or what creates large-scale change—individuals, markets, governments, institutions, or broader social forces?**

That common analytical structure makes them suitable for one bank, at least with the current corpus size.

---

## 8. Environment, Sustainability, and Urban Development

**Questions:** RT_027, RT_028, RT_029, RT_033
**RT_018 and RT_030 also overlap with this topic but are primarily education questions**

Recurring areas:

* public transportation
* urban green spaces
* durable versus energy-efficient products
* renewable energy
* environmental education
* technological environmental solutions
* factories versus individuals
* regulation versus behavioural change
* pollution, waste, conservation, and resource use
* immediate versus long-term environmental results

This is another strong, coherent bank.

---

# Recommended final set

I would therefore build **eight broad banks**:

1. **Education and Learning**
2. **Technology, AI, and Automation**
3. **Digital Media, Communication, and Relationships**
4. **Business, Marketing, and Consumer Behaviour**
5. **Work, Careers, and Professional Life**
6. **Society, Culture, Identity, and Human Behaviour**
7. **Government, Economics, History, and International Institutions**
8. **Environment, Sustainability, and Urban Development**

The important conclusion is that these should probably **not be called “idea banks” in exactly the speaking sense**. The corpus supports creating **topic-specific reasoning banks**: stakeholders, consequences, trade-offs, causal mechanisms, examples, and evaluative criteria that repeatedly appear within each domain. The students would then use those resources to construct a position tailored to the precise question.
