CHAPTER 00

What a non-finite clause is

A non-finite clause contains a verb form such as doing, having done, done, or to do, but it does not carry an ordinary tense of its own. It normally depends on another part of the sentence for its time, subject, or interpretation.

FINITE

He was trying to stop her.

The verb carries tense through was.

NON-FINITE

He kicked and punched, trying to stop her.

The time of trying is understood from kicked and punched.

KEY POINT

The -ing form is not automatically a continuous tense. A continuous tense needs a form of be: is working, was working, will be working. In She left, smiling, the word smiling begins a shortened side clause.

English often leaves the connector unstated

A shortened clause can carry relationships such as while, because, by doing so, thereby, although, if, after, or in order to. The grammar does not always identify the relationship directly. The reader infers it from logic and context.

doingoften simultaneous, causal, resultative, conditional, or intentional
having doneusually completed before the main event
doneusually passive meaning or a resulting state
to dooften purpose, possibility, evaluation, obligation, or later result
noun + doing/donea side situation with its own stated subject

Are non-finite clauses dependent?

In normal sentence structure, yes. They usually rely on another clause or phrase. They can appear alone in signs, headings, or dramatic fragments—No smoking, To be continued, To think that I trusted him!—but those are special uses.

CHAPTER 01

-ing clauses: one form, many relationships

An -ing clause usually attaches a secondary situation to the main clause. It has no tense by itself. In She walked home, listening to music, both actions are understood as past because the main verb is walked.

1. While or as: simultaneous action

The two actions happen together or overlap.

EXAMPLES

She sat by the window, watching the rain.

Fuller meaning: She sat by the window while she watched the rain.

The children crossed the field, laughing loudly.

Fuller meaning: They laughed as they crossed the field.

He drove through the city, listening to the debate.

Fuller meaning: He listened to the debate while he drove.

The professor paced across the stage, developing her argument.

Fuller meaning: She developed her argument while pacing.

2. Because: reason

The side clause explains why the main action happened.

EXAMPLES

Knowing that the road was closed, we took another route.

Fuller meaning: Because we knew the road was closed, we took another route.

Feeling exhausted, she cancelled the appointment.

Fuller meaning: Because she felt exhausted, she cancelled it.

Not understanding the instructions, I asked for clarification.

Fuller meaning: Because I did not understand them, I asked for clarification.

Wanting to avoid bias, the researchers concealed the study’s purpose.

Fuller meaning: Because they wanted to avoid bias, they concealed it.

3. By doing this: method or means

The -ing clause can explain how someone achieves a result. The word by is often kept because it makes the relationship unmistakable.

EXAMPLES

She improved her pronunciation by recording herself.

Fuller meaning: Recording herself was the method she used.

The editor strengthened the article by removing unsupported claims.

Fuller meaning: Removing the claims made the article stronger.

They reduced costs by combining two approval stages.

Fuller meaning: Combining the stages was the method.

He gained their trust by admitting his earlier mistake.

Fuller meaning: His admission helped him gain their trust.

4. Thereby, and so, or as a result

Here the main event causes the -ing

RESULT

The storm damaged the power lines, leaving thousands of homes without electricity.

Fuller meaning: The damage caused thousands of homes to lose electricity.

She dropped the glass, breaking it into several pieces.

Fuller meaning: Dropping the glass caused it to break.

The company concealed the losses, misleading its investors.

Fuller meaning: The concealment caused investors to be misled.

The river changed course, isolating the northern settlement.

Fuller meaning: The change in course caused the settlement’s isolation.

5. Intention: trying, hoping, looking for

The side clause can describe a person’s aim. The aim may succeed or fail.

EXAMPLES

He kicked and punched, trying to stop her.

Fuller meaning: He kicked and punched in an attempt to stop her.

She raised her hand, hoping to attract the teacher’s attention.

Fuller meaning: She hoped her action would attract attention.

The investigators returned, looking for overlooked evidence.

Fuller meaning: Their purpose was to look for evidence.

He smiled, trying to hide his disappointment.

Fuller meaning: The smile was an attempt to hide disappointment.

6. Significance or broader effect

The final clause can explain why the information in the main clause matters.

EXAMPLES

Wind currents transport moisture and influence temperature, playing a key role in weather patterns.

Fuller meaning: These functions mean that wind currents play a key role.

Trees absorb carbon dioxide, helping to regulate the climate.

Fuller meaning: Their absorption contributes to climate regulation.

The law protects workers from arbitrary dismissal, providing an important form of security.

Fuller meaning: The protection provides security.

The railway links remote towns, giving residents easier access to major cities.

Fuller meaning: The connection gives residents better access.

7. If: condition

The side clause can describe the condition under which the main statement is true. Sometimes both if and by are reasonable interpretations.

EXAMPLES

Using this method, you can complete the task more quickly.

Fuller meaning: If you use this method, you can complete it more quickly.

Looking closely at the figures, you can see a downward trend.

Fuller meaning: If you look closely, you can see the trend.

Adding more water, you can make the sauce thinner.

Fuller meaning: By or if you add water, you can make it thinner.

Adopting a longer time frame, we may reach a different conclusion.

Fuller meaning: If we adopt one, our conclusion may change.

8. Although: contrast

Context can turn the same form into a contrast. The main action must go against what we would normally expect.

COMPARE

Knowing that it was dangerous, he stopped.

Fuller meaning: Because he knew it was dangerous, he stopped.

Knowing that it was dangerous, he continued.

Fuller meaning: Although he knew it was dangerous, he continued.

Lacking formal experience, she performed exceptionally well.

Fuller meaning: Although she lacked experience, she performed well.

Recognizing the proposal’s weaknesses, he still supported it.

Fuller meaning: Although he recognized the weaknesses, he supported it.

9. When or immediate sequence

The first action may happen immediately before the main event. English often uses an ordinary -ing clause when the order is obvious and nearly simultaneous.

EXAMPLES

Opening the door, she noticed a strange smell.

Fuller meaning: When she opened the door, she noticed the smell.

Hearing the alarm, everyone left the building.

Fuller meaning: When they heard it, they left.

Turning the corner, he saw the damaged bridge.

Fuller meaning: As soon as he turned the corner, he saw it.

Reaching the summit, the climbers stopped to rest.

Fuller meaning: When they reached the summit, they stopped.

How the reader chooses the meaning

The form alone does not decide. Compare She opened the window, smiling (simultaneous action), She opened the window, letting cold air in (result), and She opened the window, hoping to cool the room (intention). Vocabulary and real-world logic supply the connector.

COMMON ERROR

The understood subject is normally the subject of the main clause. Walking through the park, the rain began wrongly suggests that the rain was walking. Write: Walking through the park, I noticed that it had begun to rain.

CHAPTER 02

Perfect participial clauses: having done

The pattern having + past participle presents one action as completed before the main action. It is especially useful when the earlier event must be clear.

1. After: completed sequence

EXAMPLES

Having finished the report, she sent it to her manager.

Fuller meaning: After she had finished the report, she sent it.

Having locked the door, he put the key in his pocket.

Fuller meaning: He locked the door first and then put the key away.

Having examined the scans, the surgeon recommended a different procedure.

Fuller meaning: The examination happened before the recommendation.

Having heard all the testimony, the panel withdrew to deliberate.

Fuller meaning: The panel heard everything before it withdrew.

2. Because: an earlier cause

The completed event may also explain the main event. Frequently, both after and because are true.

EXAMPLES

Having missed the last bus, we walked home.

Fuller meaning: Because we had missed it, we had to walk.

Having worked with the software before, Farid identified the fault immediately.

Fuller meaning: His earlier experience explains his speed.

Having underestimated demand, the retailer ran out of stock.

Fuller meaning: The earlier miscalculation caused the shortage.

Having seen similar policies fail, Mina opposed the proposal.

Fuller meaning: Her earlier experience explains her opposition.

3. Once: a prerequisite has been completed

EXAMPLES

Having identified the contaminant, the chemists designed a removal process.

Fuller meaning: Once they had identified it, they could design the process.

Having obtained permission, the researchers began recording interviews.

Fuller meaning: Permission came first.

Having established the criteria, the panel reviewed the applications.

Fuller meaning: The review began only after the criteria existed.

Having reached a provisional agreement, the parties discussed technical details.

Fuller meaning: The agreement allowed the next stage to begin.

4. Negative form: not having done

EXAMPLES

Not having studied the revised policy, he gave outdated advice.

Fuller meaning: Because he had not studied it, his advice was outdated.

Not having saved the document, she lost her changes.

Fuller meaning: She had failed to save it earlier.

Not having met him before, I did not recognize him.

Fuller meaning: My lack of earlier contact explains the failure to recognize him.

Not having been warned, the residents continued using the water.

Fuller meaning: They had not received a warning.

5. Passive form: having been done

Use having been + past participle when the subject received the earlier action.

EXAMPLES

Having been tested under extreme conditions, the device was approved.

Fuller meaning: The device was tested before it was approved.

Having been informed of the risk, the residents agreed to evacuate.

Fuller meaning: Someone informed them first.

Having been rejected twice, the proposal was substantially revised.

Fuller meaning: The proposal received two rejections before revision.

Having been stored at low temperature, the samples remained stable.

Fuller meaning: The samples received the action of storage.

Ordinary -ing or having done?

OVERLAP

Reading the report, he noticed several errors.

He noticed the errors during the reading.

COMPLETION FIRST

Having read the report, he wrote a detailed response.

He completed the reading before writing.

English does not require having every time one event occurs first. Reaching the station, we bought our tickets is natural because the immediate order is obvious. Having reached the station is more formal and emphasizes completion.

CHAPTER 03

Past-participle clauses: damaged, invited, surrounded

A past-participle clause usually presents the subject as receiving an action or being in a resulting state. The form itself does not necessarily refer to past time.

ACTIVE -ING

Frightening the child, the noise made her cry.

The noise causes fear.

PASSIVE / STATE

Frightened by the noise, the child began to cry.

The child receives the frightening effect.

1. Because: a passive cause or resulting state

EXAMPLES

Exhausted by the journey, she went straight to bed.

Fuller meaning: Because she was exhausted, she went to bed.

Confused by the instructions, several students asked for help.

Fuller meaning: Their confusion caused them to ask.

Encouraged by the response, he expanded the project.

Fuller meaning: The response gave him encouragement.

Alarmed by the decline, investors sold their shares.

Fuller meaning: Their alarm explains the sale.

2. After or when: a passive event

EXAMPLES

Released from custody, the journalist spoke publicly.

Fuller meaning: After he was released, he spoke.

Invited to the final interview, the applicants received instructions.

Fuller meaning: After they were invited, they received instructions.

Viewed from above, the island resembles a crescent.

Fuller meaning: When it is viewed from above, it has that appearance.

Read aloud, the poem’s rhythm becomes clearer.

Fuller meaning: When the poem is read aloud, the rhythm is clearer.

3. If: condition

EXAMPLES

Handled carefully, the equipment should last for years.

Fuller meaning: If it is handled carefully, it should last.

Stored in a cool place, the medicine remains effective.

Fuller meaning: If it is stored there, it remains effective.

Presented clearly, the argument will be more persuasive.

Fuller meaning: If it is presented clearly, it will be stronger.

Left untreated, the problem may become serious.

Fuller meaning: If it is not treated, it may worsen.

4. Although: contrast

EXAMPLES

Warned repeatedly, she refused to alter the policy.

Fuller meaning: Although she was warned, she refused.

Rejected by most contemporaries, the theory later transformed the field.

Fuller meaning: Although people rejected it, it later became influential.

Given very little time, the team produced a rigorous report.

Fuller meaning: Despite receiving little time, the team succeeded.

Surrounded by critics, the director defended the decision calmly.

Fuller meaning: Although critics surrounded her, she remained calm.

5. Background condition

Sometimes the clause simply describes the subject’s state during the main event.

EXAMPLES

She stood at the entrance, surrounded by reporters.

Fuller meaning: Reporters surrounded her while she stood there.

The village lay below us, covered in snow.

Fuller meaning: Snow covered the village at that time.

He listened to the verdict, visibly shaken.

Fuller meaning: He was visibly shaken while listening.

The documents remained on the desk, untouched for days.

Fuller meaning: No one touched them during that period.

6. After a noun: a shortened relative clause

A past-participle clause can identify or describe a noun directly.

COMPARE

The artifacts recovered from the shipwreck require conservation.

Fuller meaning: The artifacts that were recovered from the shipwreck require conservation.

The method developed by the team is now widely used.

Fuller meaning: The method that was developed by the team is now widely used.

The evidence presented during the appeal changed the judgment.

Fuller meaning: The evidence that was presented changed it.

The villages affected by the drought received assistance.

Fuller meaning: The villages that were affected received assistance.

REMEMBER

Make sure the opening description logically belongs to the main subject. Damaged by the storm, the authorities closed the bridge wrongly describes the authorities as damaged. Write: Damaged by the storm, the bridge was closed by the authorities.

CHAPTER 04

Infinitival clauses: to do, to have done, to be done

An infinitival clause normally begins with to + base verb. Its meaning comes from the structure around it. It frequently expresses purpose, a later result, possibility, obligation, evaluation, or an earlier action.

1. Purpose: in order to

EXAMPLES

She visited the archive to examine the original correspondence.

Fuller meaning: She visited because she wanted to examine it.

We left early to avoid the traffic.

Fuller meaning: Avoiding traffic was our purpose.

The researchers repeated the trial to verify the result.

Fuller meaning: Verification was the purpose of repetition.

He lowered the blinds to reduce the glare.

Fuller meaning: He wanted the glare to decrease.

SUBJECT CHECK

The person or thing carrying out the purpose should normally be the main subject. To improve the school, more money is needed sounds as though the money has the intention. Clearer: To improve the school, the government needs to provide more money.

2. Later discovery or result

EXAMPLES

She returned home to find the door open.

Fuller meaning: She returned and then discovered that it was open.

We reached the station to learn that the train had been cancelled.

Fuller meaning: The news came when we reached the station.

He opened the box to find it empty.

Fuller meaning: Opening the box led to the discovery.

The inspectors entered the warehouse to see that the records had disappeared.

Fuller meaning: They entered and then saw the result.

3. Unexpected result: only to

Only to often communicates disappointment, irony, or an outcome that defeats the earlier effort.

EXAMPLES

He studied for months, only to fail the examination.

Fuller meaning: He studied, but the final outcome was failure.

She hurried to the station, only to discover that the train had left.

Fuller meaning: Her effort ended in a disappointing discovery.

They developed the product for years, only to see it rejected.

Fuller meaning: The later result contradicted their hopes.

We drove for two hours, only to find the road closed.

Fuller meaning: The journey ended in an unexpected obstacle.

4. Too ... to and enough to

COMPARE

The evidence was too weak to support a definite conclusion.

Fuller meaning: It was so weak that it could not support one.

The corridor was too narrow for two people to pass.

Fuller meaning: Its narrowness made passage impossible.

The sample was large enough to detect a modest effect.

Fuller meaning: Its size made detection possible.

The explanation was clear enough for non-specialists to follow.

Fuller meaning: It had sufficient clarity.

5. Reaction after an adjective

EXAMPLES

I was relieved to hear that no one had been injured.

Fuller meaning: Hearing the news caused relief.

She was surprised to see him there.

Fuller meaning: Seeing him caused surprise.

We were disappointed to learn that the event had been cancelled.

Fuller meaning: The information caused disappointment.

The curator was delighted to discover the missing panel.

Fuller meaning: The discovery caused delight.

6. Judgment about someone’s action

EXAMPLES

He was wise to keep a written record.

Fuller meaning: Keeping the record was wise of him.

She was brave to challenge the consensus.

Fuller meaning: Her challenge was brave.

The agency was careless to publish unchecked figures.

Fuller meaning: Publishing them without checking was careless.

They were sensible to request an independent inspection.

Fuller meaning: The request was a sensible action.

7. After a noun: purpose, function, task, or obligation

EXAMPLES

I have several emails to answer.

Fuller meaning: I have several emails that I need to answer.

The committee has three objections to address.

Fuller meaning: It must address three objections.

They designed a sensor to measure air pressure.

Fuller meaning: The sensor’s intended function is measurement.

The laboratory needs a specialist to maintain the equipment.

Fuller meaning: It needs someone who can maintain it.

8. Giving the infinitive its own subject: for someone to do

EXAMPLES

It is essential for every participant to give informed consent.

Fuller meaning: Every participant is the subject of giving.

We arranged for an auditor to examine the accounts.

Fuller meaning: The auditor, not we, performs the examination.

The door was too heavy for the child to open.

Fuller meaning: The child is the understood opener.

It would be unrealistic for one department to absorb the entire cost.

Fuller meaning: One department is the subject of absorb.

9. Perfect infinitive: to have done

This form places the infinitive action before the time of the main verb.

EXAMPLES

She seems to have forgotten the meeting.

Fuller meaning: It seems that she forgot it earlier.

He claims to have witnessed the accident.

Fuller meaning: He claims that he witnessed it.

The archive appears to have lost several boxes.

Fuller meaning: The loss seems to have happened earlier.

I am glad to have met you.

Fuller meaning: The meeting has already occurred.

10. Passive infinitive: to be done and to have been done

EXAMPLES

The machine needs to be repaired.

Fuller meaning: Someone needs to repair the machine.

She expects to be invited.

Fuller meaning: She expects someone to invite her.

The painting is believed to have been removed during the renovation.

Fuller meaning: People believe that someone removed it earlier.

The samples appear to have been contaminated.

Fuller meaning: It appears that contamination happened earlier.

11. Commenting on the whole sentence

Some infinitive expressions organize the message or state the speaker’s attitude rather than giving a literal purpose.

EXAMPLES

To be honest, the compromise satisfied neither side.

Fuller meaning: The speaker presents the statement honestly.

To put it simply, the plan costs too much.

Fuller meaning: The speaker is simplifying the explanation.

To make matters worse, the backup system also failed.

Fuller meaning: The phrase adds a worse development.

To summarize, the policy created more problems than it solved.

Fuller meaning: The phrase introduces a summary.

CHAPTER 05

Absolute constructions: a side clause with its own subject

Most participial clauses borrow the subject of the main clause: Walking home, Sara called her brother means that Sara was walking. An absolute construction states a different subject inside the side clause: Her phone battery dying, Sara ended the call.

SHARED SUBJECT

Walking down the street, Ali saw an old friend.

Ali was walking.

OWN SUBJECT

His dog walking beside him, Ali saw an old friend.

The dog was walking.

1. Own subject + -ing

EXAMPLES

The wind blowing strongly, we remained indoors.

Fuller meaning: Because the wind was blowing strongly, we remained indoors.

His voice trembling, he began the speech.

Fuller meaning: His voice was trembling as he began.

The children sleeping upstairs, we spoke quietly.

Fuller meaning: Because or while they were sleeping, we spoke quietly.

The water rising rapidly, residents moved to higher ground.

Fuller meaning: The rising water created the reason or background.

2. Own subject + past participle

EXAMPLES

The work completed, the team went home.

Fuller meaning: After the work had been completed, the team went home.

The main entrance blocked, visitors used the eastern gate.

Fuller meaning: Because it was blocked, they used another entrance.

All the tickets sold, the organizers added another performance.

Fuller meaning: Because the tickets had all been sold, they added one.

The evidence examined, the court announced its decision.

Fuller meaning: After the evidence had been examined, the court announced it.

3. Own subject + adjective

A form of be is understood but omitted.

EXAMPLES

Her face pale, she stepped away from the window.

Fuller meaning: Her face was pale as she stepped away.

The room silent, the chairperson began.

Fuller meaning: Because the room was silent, the chairperson began.

His eyes closed, he listened to the engine.

Fuller meaning: His eyes were closed while he listened.

The streets empty, the old quarter felt unfamiliar.

Fuller meaning: The empty streets formed the background.

4. Own subject + prepositional phrase

EXAMPLES

His hands in his pockets, Omid crossed the courtyard.

Fuller meaning: His hands were in his pockets while he crossed.

The director out of the office, no authorization could be issued.

Fuller meaning: Because the director was away, authorization was impossible.

A folder under her arm, the researcher entered the archive.

Fuller meaning: She entered while a folder was under her arm.

The documents on the table, every delegate could inspect them.

Fuller meaning: Because the documents were available there, inspection was possible.

5. Absolute constructions with with

Adding with is common and often sounds more natural in everyday English. The structure may express background, cause, condition, or contrast.

EXAMPLES

With the roads covered in snow, we decided not to travel.

Fuller meaning: Because the roads were covered, we stayed.

With everyone watching, he attempted the difficult jump.

Fuller meaning: Everyone watched while he attempted it.

With enough funding, the project could be completed next year.

Fuller meaning: If sufficient funding is available, completion is possible.

With all his experience, he still made a serious mistake.

Fuller meaning: Although he had extensive experience, he made the mistake.

6. Perfect absolute: own subject + having done

This form makes the earlier completed situation explicit while preserving a separate subject.

EXAMPLES

The negotiations having failed, both sides prepared for a dispute.

Fuller meaning: Because the negotiations had failed, both sides prepared.

The deadline having passed, the agency issued an extension.

Fuller meaning: The deadline passed before the extension.

The witnesses having been questioned, the hearing moved to documents.

Fuller meaning: After the witnesses had been questioned, the next stage began.

The committee having reached no consensus, the vote was postponed.

Fuller meaning: Because it had reached no consensus, the vote was postponed.

What relationship does an absolute construction express?

Just like other shortened clauses, it does not always contain an explicit connector. It may mean while, because, after, if, or although. Logic decides:

  • The lights flickering, we stopped the experiment. → because
  • Her hands shaking, she signed the form. → while / background
  • The vote completed, the chair announced the result. → after
  • With more evidence available, the court might reconsider. → if
  • With all his preparation, he still felt nervous. → although
STYLE NOTE

Absolute constructions without with can sound formal or literary. The with version is often more natural in conversation and general writing: With the roads closed, we stayed home.

REFERENCE

Final decision guide

Use doing when...

the side event overlaps, explains a reason, gives a method, shows a result, describes an intention, or supplies a condition or contrast.

Use having done when...

you need to make it clear that the side event was completed before the main event.

Use done when...

the subject receives an action or is described in a resulting state.

Use to do when...

you express purpose, later discovery, an unexpected result, possibility, obligation, function, reaction, or judgment.

Use an absolute construction when...

the side situation needs its own stated subject rather than borrowing the main subject.

FINAL CHECK

Do not reduce a clause mechanically. First preserve the subject, time order, active or passive meaning, and logical relationship. A shorter sentence is useful only when it remains unambiguous.
