Chapter index

Chapter 01

The Big Picture: Four Connected Ideas

The words in this guide belong to a connected system. They help us add information, agree with another speaker, choose between alternatives, exclude alternatives, and show that something is excessive.

Addition

too, also, as well

These add a similar positive fact.

Negative addition

either, neither, nor

These add, combine, or refer to negative ideas.

Alternatives

either...or, neither...nor, whether...or

These organize two choices or possibilities.

Key point

The same word can have more than one grammatical job. For example, either can mean “one of two,” can introduce two alternatives, and can appear at the end of a negative sentence to mean “also not.” Always identify the structure, not just the word.

Practice 1: Identify the general meaning

  1. I enjoyed the documentary too.
  2. Neither answer is convincing.
  3. You may either submit a report or give a presentation.
  4. The task is too complicated for beginners.
Answer key and explanations
  1. Addition. Too means “also.”
  2. Negative reference to two. Neither means “not the first and not the second.”
  3. Alternatives. Either...or presents two choices.
  4. Excessive degree. Too means “more complicated than is acceptable or manageable.”
Chapter 02

Inversion: A Tool You Will Use Repeatedly

Inversion means putting an auxiliary verb or the verb be before the subject. English uses this order in questions and in several formal or emphatic structures. Learning inversion now will make later sections much easier, especially so do I, neither do I, nor did she, and sentence-opening not only.

2.1 The basic inversion formula

Trigger + auxiliary / modal / be + subject + rest of clause

The “trigger” may be a question word, so, neither, nor, or a negative expression placed at the beginning.

2.2 If the original clause contains be

Move the correct form of be before the subject. Do not add do.

Examples

She is ready. → So is she.

They were not informed. → Neither were they.

2.3 If the original clause contains a modal

Move the modal before the subject. Modals include can, could, may, might, must, should, will, would, and similar verbs.

I can help. → So can I.

She should not leave. → Neither should he.

2.4 If the original clause already contains an auxiliary

Move that auxiliary before the subject. Common auxiliaries include forms of have in perfect tenses and forms of be in continuous or passive structures.

They have finished. → So have we.

She was being observed. → So was he.

The results had not been published. → Nor had the methods been explained.

2.5 If there is no auxiliary: use do, does, or did

Simple present and simple past statements often contain no visible auxiliary. In that case, add the correct form of do. The main verb returns to its base form after do/does/did.

Original tenseAuxiliary for inversionExample
Simple present with I/you/we/theydoI agree. → So do I.
Simple present with he/she/itdoesShe agrees. → So does he.
Simple pastdidThey agreed. → So did we.
Common error

Do not repeat the past or third-person ending after did or does: So did she agree, not So did she agreed; So does he study, not So does he studies.

Practice 2: Build the inversion

Complete each response.

  1. A: I am exhausted. B: So ___ I.
  2. A: Sara studies biology. B: So ___ Mina.
  3. A: They have not replied. B: Neither ___ we.
  4. A: He could not attend. B: Nor ___ his supervisor.
  5. A: The article did not explain the cause. B: Nor ___ it propose a solution.
Answer key and explanations
  1. am. The original verb is be.
  2. does. Simple present with a third-person singular subject requires does.
  3. have. The present perfect already contains the auxiliary have.
  4. could. Move the modal before the new subject.
  5. did. The second clause is simple past, so use did and the base verb propose.
Chapter 03

Additive Too, Also, and As Well

3.1 Too meaning “also”

At the end of a positive statement, too adds a second similar fact. It does not normally introduce the first fact; it tells us that the same idea is true for another person, thing, or situation.

I enjoyed the lecture too.

The new policy affects graduate students too.

She has completed the survey too.

In formal writing, too may appear after the subject and be separated by commas. This gives the added subject special emphasis.

The researchers, too, questioned the reliability of the data.

3.2 Position of also

Also usually appears before the main verb, but after the verb be. With an auxiliary or modal, it normally appears after the first auxiliary.

She also studies chemistry.

She is also interested in physics.

She has also completed the laboratory course.

She can also explain the procedure.

3.3 As well

As well normally appears at the end of a clause. Its meaning is close to additive too, although it can sound slightly more neutral or formal in some contexts.

The course develops writing skills as well.

Practice 3: Choose and position the additive

  1. Rewrite with too: “Nina attended the workshop. Reza attended the workshop.”
  2. Place also correctly: “The method is reliable.”
  3. Place also correctly: “The team has repeated the experiment.”
  4. Rewrite formally with parenthetical too: “The reviewers questioned the conclusion.”
Answer key and explanations
  1. Reza attended the workshop too. End-position too adds the same positive fact.
  2. The method is also reliable. Also follows the verb be.
  3. The team has also repeated the experiment. Also follows the first auxiliary.
  4. The reviewers, too, questioned the conclusion. The commas mark the formal parenthetical position.
Chapter 04

Negative Additive Either

At the end of a negative clause, either means “also not.” It adds a second negative fact. This use is different from the “one of two” meaning introduced later.

Positive

I agree too.

She is ready too.

Negative

I do not agree either.

She is not ready either.

In standard formal English, do not normally use additive too after a negative clause. Use either.

Common error

I do not understand too. → I do not understand either.

Practice 4: Complete with too or either

  1. Mina enjoyed the seminar, and I did ___.
  2. Mina did not enjoy the seminar, and I did not ___.
  3. The proposal is not affordable, and it is not practical ___.
  4. The revised version addresses the cost problem and improves accessibility ___.
Answer key and explanations
  1. too: the clause is positive.
  2. either: the clause is negative.
  3. either: it adds a second negative description.
  4. too: it adds a second positive benefit.
Chapter 05

Agreement: So Do I, Neither Do I, and Related Forms

Agreement responses show that the same statement is true for another person. Positive agreement begins with so; negative agreement begins with neither or, more formally, nor.

Positive: So + auxiliary/modal/be + subject Negative: Neither/Nor + auxiliary/modal/be + subject
StatementFull responseInverted response
I like it.I do too.So do I.
I am tired.I am too.So am I.
I have finished.I have too.So have I.
I do not like it.I do not either.Neither do I.
I am not tired.I am not either.Neither am I.
I cannot attend.I cannot either.Neither can I.

Informal spoken English also uses Me too and Me neither. These are natural in conversation, but the full patterns give you more grammatical control and are useful in formal speaking practice.

Practice 5: Respond with agreement

  1. I enjoy historical documentaries. (positive agreement)
  2. I am not convinced. (negative agreement)
  3. I have completed the survey. (positive agreement)
  4. I could not identify the main cause. (negative agreement)
  5. The researchers had underestimated the cost. (positive agreement)
Answer key and explanations
  1. So do I. Simple present requires do.
  2. Neither am I. Move am before the subject.
  3. So have I. Reuse the perfect auxiliary have.
  4. Neither could I. Reuse the modal could.
  5. So had I. Reuse the past-perfect auxiliary had.
Chapter 06

Either as a Determiner or Pronoun

When referring to two people or things, either means “one or the other.” As a determiner, it comes before a singular count noun. As a pronoun, it stands alone because the noun is already understood.

Determiner

Either answer is acceptable.

You may use either method.

Pronoun

Either is acceptable.

I can work with either.

The noun after determiner either is singular: either answer, not either answers.

Practice 6: Use determiner or pronoun either

  1. There are two routes. ___ route will take about an hour.
  2. Would you prefer the morning class or the evening class? ___ is fine.
  3. Correct the error: “Either solutions would work.”
  4. Rewrite without repeating the noun: “Either proposal could be adopted.”
Answer key and explanations
  1. Either: it modifies singular route.
  2. Either: it stands alone as a pronoun.
  3. Either solution would work. The noun must be singular.
  4. Either could be adopted. The noun is understood from context.
Chapter 07

Neither as a Determiner or Pronoun

Neither means “not the first and not the second.” It normally refers to two people or things. Like either, it can modify a singular noun or stand alone.

Determiner

Neither answer is correct.

Neither student responded.

Pronoun

Neither is correct.

I invited both students, but neither responded.

In formal English, the verb is usually singular because neither means “not either one”: Neither is, neither has, neither works.

Practice 7: Use neither accurately

  1. ___ explanation accounts for all the evidence.
  2. Both candidates were interviewed, but ___ was selected.
  3. Correct the error: “Neither students has submitted the form.”
  4. Choose the more formal verb: “Neither of the theories (is/are) persuasive.”
Answer key and explanations
  1. Neither: it modifies singular explanation.
  2. neither: it is a pronoun replacing “neither candidate.”
  3. Neither student has submitted the form. Use a singular noun.
  4. is: singular agreement is the safer formal choice.
Chapter 08

Either of, Neither of, and Both of

Use these forms before plural noun phrases or object pronouns. The noun phrase after of is plural, even though either and neither select or reject one member at a time.

either of the options / either of them

neither of the students / neither of us

both of the methods / both of these

In careful formal writing, use singular agreement with either of and neither of: Either of the methods is acceptable. Plural verbs occur in everyday English, especially after neither of, but singular agreement is clearer for formal TOEFL writing.

Practice 8: Complete the structure

  1. Either of the two solutions ___ acceptable. (is/are)
  2. Neither of ___ understood the instruction. (we/us)
  3. Both ___ the articles discuss migration. (of/from)
  4. Correct the error: “Neither of the student was prepared.”
Answer key and explanations
  1. is: formal singular agreement.
  2. us: a pronoun after a preposition must be in object form.
  3. of: the structure is both of + plural noun phrase.
  4. Neither of the students was prepared. The noun after of must be plural.
Chapter 09

Both...and, Either...or, and Neither...nor

These are correlative conjunctions: paired words that connect grammatically equal elements. They may connect nouns, adjectives, verbs, phrases, or entire clauses.

StructureCore meaningExample
both X and YX and Y are includedBoth the lecture and the article were useful.
either X or Yone alternativeYou may either revise the essay or replace it.
neither X nor Ynot X and not YThe explanation is neither clear nor accurate.

9.1 Parallel structure

The material after the first connector should match the grammatical form after the second connector. Parallel structure makes the relationship clear and balanced.

Correct: either to postpone the trial or to reduce its scope

Correct: neither economically realistic nor politically acceptable

Incorrect: either postponing the trial or to reduce its scope

9.2 Position controls meaning

Place either or neither immediately before the first element being contrasted. Moving the connector can change what the listener understands as the two alternatives.

She either forgot the appointment or decided not to attend. (two actions)

She forgot either the appointment time or the room number. (two objects)

9.3 Subject–verb agreement

With both...and, the subject is plural. With either...or and neither...nor, the verb usually agrees with the nearer subject.

Both the director and the assistants are attending.

Either the professor or the students are presenting.

Either the students or the professor is presenting.

9.4 Avoid double negatives

Neither already contains a negative meaning. Do not usually combine it with another ordinary negative.

I do not like neither option.

I like neither option. / I do not like either option.

Practice 9: Paired conjunctions

  1. Complete: The course develops ___ accuracy ___ confidence.
  2. Complete: You may ___ submit the form online ___ deliver it in person.
  3. Correct the parallelism: “She neither explained the delay nor offering an apology.”
  4. Choose the verb: “Neither the assistants nor the director (was/were) informed.”
  5. Rewrite without a double negative: “We did not accept neither proposal.”
Answer key and explanations
  1. both...and: both benefits are included.
  2. either...or: two alternatives are offered.
  3. She neither explained the delay nor offered an apology. Both connected items are past-tense verb phrases.
  4. was: the nearer subject, director, is singular.
  5. We accepted neither proposal or We did not accept either proposal.

9.5 Fifty advanced examples

The following sentences show either...or and neither...nor connecting different structures: subjects, objects, complements, verb phrases, infinitives, participles, subordinate clauses, and complete propositions.

  1. Either the underlying assumptions are flawed, or the statistical model has been applied beyond the conditions for which it was designed.
  2. Neither the unusually high response rate nor the apparent consistency of the findings eliminates the possibility of sampling bias.
  3. The committee must either revise the eligibility criteria or explain why the current exclusions remain ethically defensible.
  4. The intervention was neither sufficiently targeted to address the root cause nor flexible enough to accommodate regional variation.
  5. Either by reducing administrative duplication or by reallocating existing personnel, the institution could improve efficiency without cutting essential services.
  6. Neither accepting the preliminary estimate at face value nor rejecting it without analysis would constitute a responsible response.
  7. The discrepancy may reflect either an error in measurement or a genuine shift in participant behaviour.
  8. The proposal neither identifies who would bear the additional cost nor specifies how compliance would be monitored.
  9. Either what the witnesses remembered was incomplete, or what they reported was influenced by later discussion.
  10. Neither what the policy promises nor what its advocates assume is fully supported by the available evidence.
  11. The researchers could either repeat the experiment under more controlled conditions or acknowledge the limits of causal interpretation.
  12. The revised framework is neither a minor extension of the earlier theory nor a complete rejection of its central principles.
  13. Either the apparent improvement resulted from the treatment itself, or participants changed their behaviour because they knew they were being observed.
  14. Neither because the issue is politically sensitive nor because the data are difficult to collect should it be excluded from serious analysis.
  15. The regulation applies either when an organisation stores personal data or when it transfers such data to an external provider.
  16. The results were neither as decisive as the press release suggested nor as inconclusive as several critics later claimed.
  17. Either to preserve comparability across studies or to reflect local realities, researchers must make their methodological priorities explicit.
  18. Neither having secured initial funding nor having recruited experienced staff guarantees that the project will remain sustainable.
  19. The author presents the conflict as arising either from incompatible values or from unequal access to political influence.
  20. The final decision was shaped neither exclusively by scientific advice nor solely by public pressure.
  21. Either the agency underestimated the scale of the problem, or it overestimated the capacity of local institutions to respond.
  22. Neither the frequency with which a claim is repeated nor the confidence with which it is expressed makes it reliable.
  23. The university should either provide the promised facilities before enrolment or allow affected students to withdraw without penalty.
  24. The material is neither too technical for advanced undergraduates nor sufficiently elementary for students with no background in the field.
  25. Either those responsible for the error were not consulted, or their warnings were ignored during the final review.
  26. Neither to dismiss the concern as irrational nor to exaggerate it for political advantage would help resolve the dispute.
  27. The new evidence may either strengthen the original interpretation or require a more fundamental revision of the theory.
  28. The report neither distinguishes short-term fluctuation from long-term change nor explains why the distinction matters.
  29. Either the system must be redesigned so that failure is less likely, or procedures must be introduced to limit the consequences when failure occurs.
  30. Neither the claim that markets always correct themselves nor the claim that regulation always succeeds survives careful historical examination.
  31. The candidate was selected either because her experience directly matched the role or because the panel valued her broader strategic perspective.
  32. The decline cannot be attributed either to a single economic shock or to one isolated policy decision.
  33. Either the participants misunderstood the scale, or the instructions failed to distinguish frequency from intensity.
  34. The changes affected neither how decisions were recorded nor who ultimately had authority to approve them.
  35. Either by accident or by design, the revised wording shifts responsibility away from the institution.
  36. Neither reducing class size without improving instruction nor introducing technology without teacher support is likely to produce lasting gains.
  37. The policy will succeed only if it is either inexpensive enough to maintain or valuable enough to justify its continuing cost.
  38. The evidence is neither so weak that it can be ignored nor so conclusive that further research is unnecessary.
  39. Either whoever drafted the contract overlooked the inconsistency, or whoever approved it assumed the contradiction would never be challenged.
  40. Neither whoever raises the objection nor whoever answers it should be allowed to redefine the terms midway through the debate.
  41. The organisation can either disclose the limitation now and preserve trust or conceal it temporarily and risk greater reputational damage later.
  42. The lecturer neither simplified the concept to the point of distortion nor presented it in a form inaccessible to non-specialists.
  43. Either the reform will redistribute existing opportunities, or it will create new ones; it cannot plausibly do neither.
  44. Neither the fact that the policy is popular nor the fact that it is inexpensive establishes that it is just.
  45. The data may have been distorted either during collection, when respondents interpreted the questions differently, or during analysis, when categories were merged.
  46. The institution neither denied that mistakes had occurred nor accepted that those mistakes revealed a systemic problem.
  47. Either we treat the anomaly as evidence against the theory, or we explain independently why the anomaly should not count.
  48. Neither had the negotiations collapsed entirely, nor had the parties reached a settlement that could survive domestic opposition.
  49. The conclusion follows only if either the initial premise is accepted without qualification or the counterexamples are excluded by definition.
  50. Neither the immediate benefits that supporters emphasise nor the long-term risks that critics predict can be evaluated without more transparent data.
Negative-verb reminder

After an already negative verb such as cannot be attributed, use either...or, not neither...nor: “The decline cannot be attributed either to a single economic shock or to one isolated policy decision.”

Chapter 10

Nor After a Negative Clause

Nor can connect a second negative clause to an earlier negative clause. This is a formal and compact alternative to adding and...not...either.

Negative clause, nor + auxiliary/modal/be + subject + rest

Ordinary coordination

The study did not identify the cause, and it did not propose a solution either.

Formal coordination

The study did not identify the cause, nor did it propose a solution.

The clause after nor uses inversion. Choose the auxiliary using the formula from Chapter 2.

She had not read the report, nor had she attended the briefing.

The plan is not affordable, nor is it practical.

He rarely participated, nor did he complete the assignments.

Practice 10: Rewrite with nor

  1. The article did not define the term, and it did not provide an example either.
  2. The measure is not accurate, and it is not reliable either.
  3. They had not tested the device, and they had not inspected the wiring either.
  4. Correct the inversion: “She did not object, nor she offered an alternative.”
Answer key and explanations
  1. The article did not define the term, nor did it provide an example.
  2. The measure is not accurate, nor is it reliable.
  3. They had not tested the device, nor had they inspected the wiring.
  4. She did not object, nor did she offer an alternative.
Chapter 11

Not Only...But Also

This pair adds two related ideas while emphasising that the second is additional or surprising. The two connected elements should be parallel.

The programme develops not only knowledge but also confidence.

She not only completed the project but also presented the results.

11.1 Inversion after sentence-opening not only

When not only begins the whole sentence and introduces a clause, that first clause uses inversion. The second clause normally has ordinary word order.

Not only + auxiliary/modal/be + subject + rest, but subject + also + verb...

Not only did she complete the project, but she also presented the results.

Not only is the policy expensive, but it is also ineffective.

Practice 11: Build balanced sentences

  1. Combine: “The course improves accuracy. It improves fluency.”
  2. Correct: “The system is not only expensive but also it wastes time.”
  3. Rewrite beginning with Not only: “She identified the flaw and proposed a solution.”
  4. Complete: “Not only ___ the sample small, but it was also unrepresentative.”
Answer key and explanations
  1. The course improves not only accuracy but also fluency.
  2. The system is not only expensive but also time-consuming, or: It not only costs too much but also wastes time.
  3. Not only did she identify the flaw, but she also proposed a solution.
  4. was: invert the verb be.

11.2 Pronouns, repeated nouns, and omitted words

Long not only...but also sentences often repeat the same noun. English usually avoids unnecessary repetition by replacing the second noun phrase with a pronoun-like expression. For plural people, the most useful formal expression is those who. In everyday conversation, the ones who is also possible.

Avoid repetition

The policy affects not only students who attend in person but also those who study online.

The grant supports not only researchers who have already published but also those who have not.

The short clause after those who may contain only an auxiliary or modal. That auxiliary replaces the repeated verb phrase. It must match the omitted structure.

Full repeated ideaNatural shortened form
students who participate / students who do not participatestudents who participate / those who do not
teachers who are experienced / teachers who are not experiencedteachers who are experienced / those who are not
applicants who have submitted / applicants who have not submittedapplicants who have submitted / those who have not
users who can read the print / users who cannot read itusers who can / those who cannot

Keep the verb and its preposition together

The pronoun does not change the grammar required by the verb. A direct transitive verb takes no added preposition, while a verb or expression that requires a preposition must keep it on both sides of the pair.

Direct object: no to

Affect someone

It affects not only those who do not run but also those who do.

It affects not only those who do not run but also to those who do.

Expression with to

Provide support to someone

It provides support not only to those who do not run but also to those who do.

Placing the preposition before each element is usually the clearest pattern:

verb + not only + preposition + X + but also + preposition + Y
Advanced substitutes for things

That of replaces a repeated singular noun phrase, and those of replaces a repeated plural noun phrase: “The cost of the new system is not only lower than that of the old one but also easier to predict.” “The results of the field study were not only clearer than those of the pilot study but also more consistent.”

Practice 11B: Pronouns and verb patterns

  1. Remove repetition: “The policy affects not only students who attend regularly but also students who do not attend regularly.”
  2. Complete naturally: “The workshop is useful not only for teachers who are new but also for ___ who are experienced.”
  3. Correct the preposition: “The rule applies not only to those who register late but also those who never register.”
  4. Correct the verb pattern: “The change affects not only those who work on campus but also to those who work remotely.”
  5. Shorten the repeated perfect verb: “The rule covers applicants who have submitted the form and applicants who have not submitted the form.” Use not only...but also.
Answer key and explanations
  1. The policy affects not only students who attend regularly but also those who do not. Those replaces students, and do not replaces do not attend regularly.
  2. those. For plural people in formal English, use those who.
  3. The rule applies not only to those who register late but also to those who never register. Repeat to for visibly balanced phrases.
  4. The change affects not only those who work on campus but also those who work remotely. Affect takes a direct object, so no to is used.
  5. The rule covers not only applicants who have submitted the form but also those who have not. Have not replaces the repeated present-perfect verb phrase.

11.3 Fifty advanced examples

These sentences demonstrate not only...but also with noun phrases, adjectives, verb phrases, infinitives, prepositional phrases, embedded clauses, and full clauses. In sentence-opening patterns, inversion appears in the first clause, while also normally follows the subject in the second clause.

  1. The revised curriculum develops not only technical competence but also the ability to evaluate evidence critically.
  2. The policy affected not only full-time employees but also contractors whose work depended on the same administrative system.
  3. The explanation is not only logically consistent but also compatible with the most recent experimental findings.
  4. The proposed intervention is not only more affordable than the existing programme but also easier to implement across rural regions.
  5. The committee not only rejected the initial recommendation but also requested a complete reassessment of the underlying evidence.
  6. The researchers not only replicated the original result but also identified the conditions under which it disappears.
  7. The new archive allows historians not only to reconstruct the sequence of events but also to compare competing interpretations of them.
  8. The course encourages students not only to identify an author's claim but also to examine the assumptions on which that claim depends.
  9. The reform was designed not only to reduce unnecessary expenditure but also to make decision-making more transparent.
  10. The technology can be used not only for detecting structural damage but also for predicting where future failures are most likely to occur.
  11. The organisation improved its performance not only by simplifying internal procedures but also by giving local offices greater authority.
  12. The theory gained influence not only because it explained the existing data but also because it generated precise, testable predictions.
  13. The decline occurred not only in regions directly exposed to the drought but also in neighbouring areas that depended on the same water system.
  14. The findings matter not only to specialists in molecular biology but also to clinicians deciding how the treatment should be administered.
  15. The debate concerns not only whether the measure is economically efficient but also whether it distributes costs fairly.
  16. The investigation examined not only what decisions had been made but also who had possessed the authority to make them.
  17. The study demonstrates not only that the two variables are associated but also that the strength of the association changes over time.
  18. The report explains not only why the earlier strategy failed but also how a revised strategy could avoid the same weaknesses.
  19. The evidence suggests not only that public attitudes have shifted but also that the reasons behind those attitudes have become more complex.
  20. The discovery altered not only how scientists understood the disease but also how they designed subsequent clinical trials.
  21. Not only did the revised procedure shorten processing time, but it also reduced the number of applications returned for correction.
  22. Not only did the witnesses provide inconsistent accounts, but they also changed important details during later interviews.
  23. Not only does the model underestimate long-term costs, but it also assumes a rate of growth that the historical data do not support.
  24. Not only does the policy fail to protect vulnerable workers, but it also creates incentives for employers to avoid formal contracts.
  25. Not only is the proposed solution technically demanding, but it is also dependent on infrastructure that many regions do not possess.
  26. Not only are the available estimates imprecise, but they are also based on samples that exclude several relevant populations.
  27. Not only was the initial interpretation incomplete, but it was also shaped by assumptions that later evidence directly contradicted.
  28. Not only were participants unaware of the study's true purpose, but they were also given information intended to influence their responses.
  29. Not only has the company failed to meet its original targets, but it has also stopped publishing the data needed to evaluate its progress.
  30. Not only have researchers documented the immediate effects of the intervention, but they have also followed participants long enough to assess whether those effects persist.
  31. Not only had the administration ignored repeated warnings, but it had also removed the safeguards that might have limited the damage.
  32. Not only had the earlier studies relied on small samples, but they had also used definitions that made meaningful comparison difficult.
  33. Not only can the device detect unusually small changes in pressure, but it can also distinguish genuine signals from background noise.
  34. Not only could the reform increase access to higher education, but it could also reduce the financial uncertainty faced by low-income students.
  35. Not only should governments publish the criteria used to allocate funding, but they should also explain how exceptions to those criteria are approved.
  36. Not only must the revised theory account for the original observations, but it must also explain the anomalies that the earlier model dismissed.
  37. Not only will the new regulation affect companies that collect personal data, but it will also apply to organisations that process such data on their behalf.
  38. Not only would the proposed merger reduce competition, but it would also place control of essential infrastructure in the hands of a single provider.
  39. Not only may the apparent improvement reflect changes in measurement, but it may also result from participants becoming familiar with the test.
  40. Not only might the policy produce unintended economic consequences, but it might also weaken the institutions responsible for correcting them.
  41. The revised account is valuable not only for what it includes but also for what it reveals about the limitations of earlier sources.
  42. The decision should be judged not only by its immediate outcome but also by the precedent it establishes for future cases.
  43. Students were asked not only to summarise the competing theories but also to determine which evidence would discriminate between them.
  44. The programme succeeded not only in attracting qualified applicants but also in retaining them after the initial training period.
  45. The agreement protects not only those who are currently employed but also those whose contracts will begin during the transition period.
  46. The mechanism explains not only how the reaction begins but also why it stops once a particular threshold has been reached.
  47. The lecturer clarified not only which interpretation was historically dominant but also why scholars have increasingly challenged it.
  48. The crisis revealed not only how dependent the region was on imported fuel but also how unevenly the resulting shortages affected different communities.
  49. The research raises questions not only about the accuracy of the published figures but also about the institutional pressures under which those figures were produced.
  50. Not only did the final ruling resolve the immediate dispute, but it also redefined the legal standard that later courts would be expected to apply.
Chapter 12

Negative and Restrictive Expressions That Trigger Inversion

The inversion pattern extends beyond neither, nor, and not only. When certain negative or restrictive expressions are moved to the beginning for emphasis, the following clause often uses question-like order.

Never have I seen such rapid growth.

Rarely do students receive immediate feedback.

Under no circumstances should personal data be shared.

Little did they know that the experiment would fail.

This is useful because it reveals one general principle: a negative or strongly restrictive expression at the beginning can pull the auxiliary in front of the subject.

Practice 12: Apply the general inversion rule

  1. Rewrite beginning with Never: “I have never encountered such resistance.”
  2. Rewrite beginning with Rarely: “Researchers rarely obtain perfectly clean data.”
  3. Correct: “Under no circumstances students should share passwords.”
  4. Rewrite beginning with Little: “They little knew how serious the problem was.”
Answer key and explanations
  1. Never have I encountered such resistance.
  2. Rarely do researchers obtain perfectly clean data.
  3. Under no circumstances should students share passwords.
  4. Little did they know how serious the problem was.
Chapter 13

Or: Choice, Reformulation, Warning, and Approximation

Or does not always mean a simple exclusive choice. Context determines the relationship.

Choice

You can submit an essay or give a presentation.

Reformulation

The animal is nocturnal, or active mainly at night.

Warning

Leave now, or you will miss the bus.

It can also express approximation: five or six days. In scientific explanation, it may present several possible causes without claiming that only one can be true.

Practice 13: Identify the relationship

  1. The process takes seven or eight hours.
  2. Reduce the file size, or the system may reject the upload.
  3. The species is arboreal, or adapted to living in trees.
  4. The decline may result from habitat loss or climate variation.
Answer key and explanations
  1. Approximation.
  2. Warning or consequence.
  3. Reformulation or explanation.
  4. Possible causes. The possibilities are not necessarily mutually exclusive.
Chapter 14

Whether...or, Whether or Not, Or Else, and Otherwise

Whether...or presents alternatives inside a larger sentence. It commonly follows verbs of uncertainty, decision, investigation, or discussion.

I do not know whether the class is online or in person.

We must decide whether to continue or stop.

Whether students agree or disagree, they should support their position.

Whether or not means that the result remains true in both cases.

The policy will continue whether or not students support it.

Or else and otherwise introduce an undesirable consequence. Otherwise often begins a new independent clause after a semicolon or period.

Submit the form today, or else your application may be delayed.

Submit the form today; otherwise, your application may be delayed.

Practice 14: Choose the connector

  1. I am unsure ___ to accept or decline.
  2. The experiment must be documented carefully; ___, others cannot reproduce it.
  3. The deadline will not change ___ or not the committee approves the request.
  4. Complete: “Back up the files, ___ you may lose your work.”
Answer key and explanations
  1. whether: use whether + to-infinitive.
  2. otherwise: it introduces the negative consequence in a new clause.
  3. whether: the pattern is whether or not.
  4. or else or simply or.
Chapter 15

Either Meaning “Each of Two”

In certain spatial expressions, either can mean “each of the two.” This use is especially common with side and end.

There were trees on either side of the road. (on both sides)

A door stood at either end of the hallway. (at both ends)

Context distinguishes this meaning from free choice.

You may sit on either side.

Choose one side.

Trees were planted on either side.

Trees were planted on both sides.

Practice 15: Interpret either

  1. There was an exit at either end of the platform.
  2. You can enter through either door.
  3. Students were seated on either side of the aisle.
Answer key and explanations
  1. Each of the two ends.
  2. One door or the other.
  3. Both sides of the aisle.
Chapter 16

Any vs. Either; None vs. Neither

For clear formal usage, use either and neither when exactly two alternatives are involved. Use any and none when the set contains three or more.

Number in the setPositive possibilityNegative quantity
Twoeitherneither
Three or moreanynone

You can choose either colour. (two colours)

You can choose any colour. (several colours)

Neither of the two explanations is convincing.

None of the four explanations is convincing.

Practice 16: Choose the precise word

  1. There are two available times. ___ would work for me.
  2. There are six available times. ___ would work for me.
  3. Of the two proposals, ___ addresses the funding problem.
  4. Of the five proposals, ___ addresses the funding problem.
Answer key and explanations
  1. Either.
  2. Any.
  3. Neither.
  4. None.
Chapter 17

Too Meaning “Excessively”

This use of too is completely different from additive too. Degree too means “more than is acceptable, desirable, useful, or possible.” It usually implies a negative result.

The article is too technical. (Its level causes a problem.)

The evidence is too limited for us to draw a conclusion.

17.1 Main patterns

PatternExample
too + adjective/adverbtoo expensive; too slowly
too much + uncountable nountoo much information
too many + plural count nountoo many variables
too + adjective + to-infinitivetoo weak to be convincing
too + adjective + for + person + to-infinitivetoo difficult for beginners to understand

Practice 17: Complete the degree structure

  1. There is ___ information to process in one hour.
  2. The survey contains ___ questions.
  3. The box is ___ heavy for one person to lift.
  4. Rewrite using a to-infinitive: “The evidence is excessively weak; therefore, it cannot support the claim.”
Answer key and explanations
  1. too much: information is uncountable.
  2. too many: questions is plural and countable.
  3. too.
  4. The evidence is too weak to support the claim.
Chapter 18

Very vs. Too; So...That vs. Too...To

Very describes a high degree. It does not automatically say that the degree creates a problem. Too says the degree crosses a limit and causes an undesirable or impossible result.

Very

The test was very difficult, but I completed it.

Too

The test was too difficult, so I could not complete it.

So...that states a result explicitly. Too...to usually implies inability or prevention.

The lecture was so interesting that I took extra notes.

The lecture was too complicated for beginners to follow.

Practice 18: Choose the intended degree

  1. The room was very/too cold, but we stayed for the entire meeting.
  2. The room was very/too cold to use safely.
  3. Complete: The explanation was so clear ___ everyone understood it.
  4. Rewrite: “The passage was so complex that the beginners could not understand it.”
Answer key and explanations
  1. very: the sentence says the meeting still continued.
  2. too: the cold prevents safe use.
  3. that.
  4. The passage was too complex for the beginners to understand.
Chapter 19

Too...To and Enough To

These structures often describe opposite sides of a limit. Too comes before an adjective or adverb. Enough comes after an adjective or adverb but before a noun.

too + adjective/adverb + to-infinitive adjective/adverb + enough + to-infinitive enough + noun + to-infinitive

The room is too dark to read.

The room is not bright enough to read.

We have enough evidence to continue.

Transformations are not always equally natural. The opposite adjective may sound awkward or change the emphasis, so focus on meaning rather than mechanically replacing every word.

Practice 19: Use the correct order

  1. Correct: “The task is enough easy to complete.”
  2. Correct: “We have time enough to repeat the test.”
  3. Rewrite with enough: “She is too young to apply.”
  4. Complete: The evidence is not strong ___ to justify the conclusion.
Answer key and explanations
  1. The task is easy enough to complete.
  2. We have enough time to repeat the test.
  3. She is not old enough to apply.
  4. enough: it follows the adjective strong.
Chapter 20

Negation, Ambiguity, and Scope

The position of a negative word can change which part of the sentence is denied. This is called the scope of negation. Advanced readers must ask: what exactly does not apply to?

20.1 Ambiguous both...not

The sentence Both students did not pass is unclear. It may mean that neither passed, or merely that not both passed. Rewrite it.

Neither student passed. (zero passed)

Not both students passed. (possibly one passed)

Only one student passed. (exactly one passed)

20.2 Negative modals and either

Compare prohibition, lack of necessity, and inability carefully.

You must not contact either professor. (contacting either one is prohibited)

You do not need to contact either professor. (contacting them is unnecessary)

You cannot choose either option. (both choices are unavailable)

Practice 20: Clarify the meaning

  1. Rewrite “Both proposals were not accepted” to mean zero proposals were accepted.
  2. Rewrite it to mean only one was accepted.
  3. Explain: “You do not have to answer either question.”
  4. Correct: “The decline cannot be explained neither by cost nor by quality.”
Answer key and explanations
  1. Neither proposal was accepted.
  2. Only one proposal was accepted or Not both proposals were accepted.
  3. It means answering the questions is not required; it does not normally prohibit answering.
  4. The decline cannot be explained either by cost or by quality. The negative verb requires either...or, not neither...nor.
Chapter 21

Comprehensive Error Clinic

IncorrectCorrectReason
I do not agree too.I do not agree either.Negative addition uses either.
Neither I do.Neither do I.Agreement requires inversion.
Neither of the student...Neither of the students...The noun after of is plural.
Either students...Either student...Determiner either takes a singular noun.
Neither John or Sara...Neither John nor Sara...The correct pair is neither...nor.
Either John nor Sara...Either John or Sara...The correct pair is either...or.
Not only she disagreed...Not only did she disagree...Sentence-opening not only triggers inversion.
The task is enough easy.The task is easy enough.Enough follows adjectives.

Practice 21: Correct the errors

  1. I cannot attend too.
  2. Neither the cost or the schedule is acceptable.
  3. Either of the answer may be correct.
  4. Nor the report explained the discrepancy.
  5. Not only the plan was expensive, but it was also ineffective.
  6. The students either could submit an essay or making a presentation.
Answer key and explanations
  1. I cannot attend either.
  2. Neither the cost nor the schedule is acceptable.
  3. Either of the answers may be correct.
  4. Nor did the report explain the discrepancy.
  5. Not only was the plan expensive, but it was also ineffective.
  6. The students could either submit an essay or make a presentation. The two verb phrases are parallel.
Chapter 22

Final Mixed Practice

Complete or correct each item. The questions become progressively more demanding.

  1. I enjoyed the reading, and my classmate did ___.
  2. I did not understand the final paragraph ___.
  3. A: I am ready. B: So ___ I.
  4. A: I have not finished. B: Neither ___ I.
  5. ___ of the two answers is acceptable.
  6. ___ of the two answers is acceptable; both are wrong.
  7. Correct: “Neither of the applicant were interviewed.”
  8. Complete: “The policy is neither affordable ___ effective.”
  9. Choose: “Either the assistants or the director (is/are) responsible.”
  10. Correct: “She either plans to revise the article or submitting it elsewhere.”
  11. Rewrite with nor: “The study did not identify the cause, and it did not test an alternative explanation either.”
  12. Rewrite beginning with Not only: “The reform reduced costs and improved access.”
  13. Correct: “Under no circumstances participants should reveal their passwords.”
  14. Choose very or too: “The data were ___ limited to support a firm conclusion.”
  15. Rewrite with enough: “The sample was too small to represent the population.”
  16. Explain the ambiguity: “Both explanations are not correct.”
  17. Correct: “The result cannot be explained neither by measurement error nor by random variation.”
  18. Combine with parallel not only...but also: “The programme trains teachers. It gives them continuing support.”
  19. Rewrite with formal inversion: “The committee had never faced a dispute of this scale.”
  20. Correct and improve: “Either the evidence was ignored, or because the reviewers misunderstood its significance.”
Final answer key and explanations
  1. too.
  2. either.
  3. am.
  4. have.
  5. Either: one of the two is acceptable.
  6. Neither: zero of the two is acceptable.
  7. Neither of the applicants was interviewed. Plural noun after of; formal singular verb.
  8. nor.
  9. is: agreement with the nearer subject.
  10. She plans either to revise the article or to submit it elsewhere.
  11. The study did not identify the cause, nor did it test an alternative explanation.
  12. Not only did the reform reduce costs, but it also improved access.
  13. Under no circumstances should participants reveal their passwords.
  14. too: the limitation prevents a firm conclusion.
  15. The sample was not large enough to represent the population.
  16. It may mean that neither explanation is correct, or that only one is correct. Rewrite as Neither explanation is correct or Only one explanation is correct.
  17. The result cannot be explained either by measurement error or by random variation.
  18. The programme not only trains teachers but also gives them continuing support.
  19. Never had the committee faced a dispute of this scale.
  20. Either the evidence was ignored, or the reviewers misunderstood its significance. Both sides must be parallel complete clauses.
Final strategy

When you meet one of these words, ask three questions: What meaning does it express? What grammatical job does it perform? Does the structure require parallelism or inversion? This three-step check prevents most errors.
