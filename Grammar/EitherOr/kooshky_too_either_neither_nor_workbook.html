How to Use This Workbook

This workbook moves from controlled agreement responses to complex academic sentence construction. Write complete answers before opening the answer key. A model answer shows one strong solution; other answers may also be correct when they preserve the intended meaning, agreement, inversion, and parallel structure.

Three checks
  1. Meaning: Are you adding a positive fact, adding a negative fact, including two items, choosing one, or rejecting both?
  2. Structure: Are the connected units grammatically parallel?
  3. Word order: Does the construction require inversion, and have you selected the correct auxiliary?

Positive agreement: So do I. · I do too. · Me too. · I also agree. · I agree as well.

Negative agreement: Neither do I. · Nor do I. · I do not either. · Me neither. · I also do not agree.

Inversion: trigger + auxiliary/modal/be + subject. If no auxiliary is present, add do/does/did.

Part 01

Conversation Responses: Positive and Negative Agreement

The second speaker must agree with the first speaker in several natural ways. Do not merely repeat the same response. Practise the inverted form, the short too/either form, the informal form, and full-clause alternatives.

Formula reminder

Positive: So + auxiliary + subject. Negative: Neither/Nor + auxiliary + subject. Match be, modals, perfect auxiliaries, passive auxiliaries, and tense.

Part 02

Stating Facts with Either, Neither, Either of, and Neither of

Transform the fact blocks into concise statements or questions. Remember: determiner either/neither takes a singular noun, while either of/neither of takes a plural noun phrase or an object pronoun.

Form reminder

Either student · either of the students · neither student · neither of them. In formal writing, singular agreement is safest after either of and neither of.

Part 2 answer key and explanations
  1. 1
    Neither student submitted the homework.
    Neither of the students submitted the homework.

    Why: Determiner neither takes a singular noun; neither of takes a plural noun phrase.

  2. 2
    Either classroom can be used.
    Either of the classrooms can be used.

    Why: Either means one of two.

  3. 3
    Did either of the students submit the homework?

    Why: In a question, either of asks whether one of the two did it.

  4. 4
    Either route will take you to the museum.
    Either of the routes will take you to the museum.

    Why: Both routes work, so a person may choose one of them.

  5. 5
    Neither explanation is persuasive.
    Neither of the explanations is persuasive.

    Why: Formal singular agreement is safest after neither.

  6. 6
    Is either seat available?
    Is either of the seats available?

    Why: Use singular seat after determiner either, but plural after either of.

  7. 7
    Neither day works for Maya.
    Neither of the days works for Maya.

    Why: Neither rejects both members of a two-item set.

  8. 8
    Either technician can reset the system.
    Either of the technicians can reset the system.

    Why: The exact technician is unimportant.

  9. 9
    Neither sample was contaminated.
    Neither of the samples was contaminated.

    Why: The singular verb agrees with the distributive meaning “not either one.”

  10. 10
    Either time is convenient for me.
    Either of the times is convenient for me.

    Why: Although both work, either focuses on choosing one.

  11. 11
    Neither of the applicants has replied.

    Why: Use a plural noun after of and formal singular agreement.

  12. 12
    Does either key open the storage room?
    Does either of the keys open the storage room?

    Why: The question asks whether one member of the pair works.

  13. 13
    Neither book is suitable for beginners.
    Neither of the books is suitable for beginners.

    Why: Do not say neither books.

  14. 14
    Either supervisor can approve the request.
    Either of the supervisors can approve the request.

    Why: The sentence does not claim that both must approve.

  15. 15
    Neither train arrived on time.

The first twenty exercises are controlled and introduce the full range of basic positions. Do not over-invert

Inversion occurs when Not only begins an entire clause. It does not occur merely because not only connects two nouns, adjectives, objects, or verb phrases inside ordinary sentence order.

Pronouns, repeated nouns, and omitted verbs

To avoid repeating a plural noun, use those who for people: “The policy affects not only students who attend in person but also those who study online.” In less formal conversation, the ones who is also possible.

The short relative clause may keep only the auxiliary that carries the contrast: those who do / those who do not, those who are / those who are not, those who have / those who have not, and those who can / those who cannot.

Keep the verb pattern unchanged. Say “It affects not only those who attend but also those who do not” because affect takes a direct object. Say “It provides support not only to those who attend but also to those who do not” because provide support requires to.
